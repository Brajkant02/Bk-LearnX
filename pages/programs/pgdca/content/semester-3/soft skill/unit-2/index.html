1. Introduction to Reading Skill

Reading skill is the ability to read written material, understand its meaning, identify important information, interpret ideas and respond correctly to questions.

An unseen passage is a passage that the student has not studied beforehand. Questions may test the main idea, details, vocabulary, synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitutions, prefixes and suffixes.

Reading

Understanding written words, sentences and ideas.

Comprehension

Understanding the meaning, facts and relationships presented in a text.

Main Objective: Read carefully, understand the central idea, locate relevant information and answer exactly what is asked.

2. How to Read an Unseen Passage

  1. Read once for overall meaning. Do not stop at every difficult word.
  2. Identify the main idea. Ask: “What is this passage mainly about?”
  3. Notice details. Pay attention to names, dates, reasons, examples, causes and effects.
  4. Read the questions carefully. Look for who, what, when, where, why and how.
  5. Locate the answer in the passage. Return to the relevant sentence or paragraph.
  6. Write clearly. Keep answers relevant and grammatically correct.
Golden Rule: Do not add personal opinions unless the question specifically asks for them. Comprehension answers should normally be based on the passage.

3. Comprehension Questions

Comprehension means understanding what has been read. Questions may test factual information, reasons, inference, main idea and vocabulary.

Type Tests Clues
Factual Direct information Who? What? When? Where?
Reason-based Cause/reason Why?
Inference Indirect meaning What can be inferred?
Main Idea Central theme What is the passage about?
Vocabulary Meaning in context What does the word mean?

Example

Passage: “Regular exercise keeps the body active and helps improve concentration.”

Question: What does regular exercise improve?

Answer: It helps improve concentration.

4. Vocabulary from the Passage

Vocabulary questions ask students to understand the meaning or usage of words appearing in a passage. Always consider the context of the sentence.

Word Meaning Example
Bright Giving light / intelligent She is a bright student.
Observe Watch carefully Scientists observe the experiment.
Rapid Very fast Technology is developing rapidly.
Essential Necessary Water is essential for life.
Preserve Protect or keep safe We should preserve natural resources.

6. Prefixes

A prefix is a letter or group of letters added to the beginning of a word to modify its meaning.

Prefix Meaning Example
un- not un + happy = unhappy
in- not in + correct = incorrect
im- not im + possible = impossible
dis- opposite/not dis + agree = disagree
re- again re + write = rewrite
pre- before pre + view = preview
mis- wrongly mis + understand = misunderstand
anti- against anti + social = antisocial
inter- between inter + national = international
sub- under/below sub + marine = submarine

7. Suffixes

A suffix is added to the end of a word and may change its meaning or grammatical form.

Suffix Function Example
-ness Forms nouns kind → kindness
-ful Full of care → careful
-less Without hope → hopeless
-er Person doing an action teach → teacher
-ly Often forms adverbs quick → quickly
-ment Forms nouns develop → development
-tion Forms nouns educate → education
-able Capable of read → readable
-ist Person associated with a field art → artist
-ship State/relationship friend → friendship
Remember: Prefix = beginning; Suffix = end.

10. Solved Unseen Passage – 1

Passage:

Time is one of the most valuable resources in human life. Money lost can sometimes be earned again, but time once lost can never be recovered. Successful people understand the importance of time and use it carefully. They prepare plans, set priorities and avoid unnecessary delays. Students can also benefit greatly by developing good time-management habits. A student who follows a proper timetable can give sufficient attention to study, rest, recreation and other responsibilities. Time management does not mean working continuously without rest. It means using available time wisely and maintaining a healthy balance between different activities. Therefore, everyone should learn to respect time and make productive use of it.

Questions

  1. Why is time considered valuable?
  2. What do successful people do with time?
  3. How can students benefit from time management?
  4. Does time management mean working continuously?
  5. Find a word meaning “important or useful”.
  6. Find the antonym of “unnecessary”.
  7. Give a suitable title.
Answers:
1. Because once lost, time can never be recovered.
2. They plan, set priorities and avoid unnecessary delays.
3. It helps students manage study, rest, recreation and responsibilities properly.
4. No. It means using time wisely and maintaining balance.
5. Valuable.
6. Necessary.
7. Importance of Time / Time Management.

11. Solved Unseen Passage – 2

Passage:

Trees are essential for maintaining life on Earth. They absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen through photosynthesis. Forests provide shelter to many animals and birds and help maintain ecological balance. Trees also reduce soil erosion, provide shade and contribute to the water cycle. Unfortunately, rapid urbanisation and excessive cutting of forests have reduced green cover in many areas. Planting trees is important, but protecting existing forests is equally necessary. Individuals, communities and governments must work together to conserve forests. A healthy environment is beneficial not only for present generations but also for the future.

Questions

  1. Why are trees essential for life?
  2. How do forests help animals?
  3. Name two benefits of trees.
  4. What has reduced green cover?
  5. Find a synonym of “protect”.
  6. Find an antonym of “excessive”.
  7. Give a suitable title.
Answers:
1. They absorb carbon dioxide, release oxygen and support ecological balance.
2. They provide shelter to animals and birds.
3. They reduce soil erosion and provide shade; they also support the water cycle.
4. Rapid urbanisation and excessive cutting of forests.
5. Conserve.
6. Limited / moderate.
7. Importance of Trees / Forest Conservation.

13. Exam Strategy

  1. Read once for the overall meaning.
  2. Identify the topic and central idea.
  3. Notice names, reasons, examples and conclusions.
  4. Read questions carefully.
  5. Find factual answers directly in the passage.
  6. Understand unfamiliar vocabulary from context.
  7. Keep answers concise and relevant.
  8. Check grammar and spelling before submission.
R-Q-F-A Technique: Read → Questions → Find information → Answer clearly.
