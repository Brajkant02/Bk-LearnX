1. Overview

When a person applies for a job, internship, training programme or professional opportunity, the employer needs a brief written summary of the applicant's education, skills, experience and achievements. Two commonly used documents for this purpose are a Curriculum Vitae (CV) and a Resume.

A covering letter is normally used along with a CV or resume to introduce the applicant, explain the purpose of the application and show why the applicant is interested in the opportunity.

CV

A detailed record of academic, professional and other relevant achievements.

Resume

A concise and targeted summary of qualifications, skills, experience and achievements.

Covering Letter

A formal letter accompanying an application and introducing the candidate to the employer.

Main Purpose

To present relevant qualifications clearly and create a professional first impression.

2. Curriculum Vitae (CV)

Definition

Curriculum Vitae is a Latin expression meaning “course of life”. A CV is a detailed document that presents a person's academic background, professional experience, skills, achievements, publications, projects and other relevant information.

Purpose of a CV

  • To provide a detailed professional and academic profile.
  • To present educational qualifications and work experience.
  • To highlight achievements, projects and specialised skills.
  • To support applications for jobs, academic positions, internships or other opportunities.
  • To help an organisation understand the applicant's background.
Important: A CV should contain information that is relevant to the purpose of the application. A detailed document does not mean that unnecessary personal information should be added.

3. Types of CV

Type Description Suitable For
Chronological CV Education and experience are generally presented in a time-based sequence, often highlighting the most recent information. Applicants with a clear academic or career history.
Functional CV Focuses more strongly on skills, abilities and achievements than on the timeline of employment. People changing careers or wanting to emphasise transferable skills.
Combination CV Combines a skills-focused section with chronological education or work experience. Applicants who want to show both skills and experience.
Academic CV Gives greater detail about academic qualifications, research, publications, conferences and teaching-related achievements. Academic, research or teaching applications.
Exam Point: The exact classification of CV types can vary among organisations and countries. The key idea is the way information is organised and what the document emphasises.

4. Important Components of a CV

Section What to Include
Contact Information Name, professional email, phone number and relevant professional links.
Career/Professional Objective A concise statement describing the type of opportunity or contribution you seek.
Education Qualifications, institutions, years and relevant academic details.
Work Experience Job title, organisation, period and important responsibilities/achievements.
Skills Technical, communication, problem-solving and other relevant skills.
Projects Relevant academic, technical or professional projects.
Achievements Awards, competitions, certifications or significant accomplishments.
Additional Sections Publications, research, volunteering, languages or interests when relevant.
References Include when requested or appropriate for the application.

5. Covering Letter

Definition

A covering letter is a formal letter submitted with a CV or resume. It introduces the applicant and explains the purpose of applying for a particular job, internship or other opportunity.

Objectives of a Covering Letter

  • Introduce yourself professionally.
  • State the position or opportunity for which you are applying.
  • Show your interest in the organisation or role.
  • Highlight a few relevant qualifications or skills.
  • Explain briefly why you may be suitable for the position.
  • Request an opportunity for further discussion or interview.

Features of a Good Covering Letter

  • Professional and polite tone.
  • Clear and concise writing.
  • Specific reference to the position.
  • Relevant skills rather than a repetition of the entire CV.
  • Correct grammar and spelling.
  • Professional closing.

6. Covering Letter Format

Part Content
Sender's Details Name, address/contact details and date, as appropriate.
Receiver's Details Name/designation, organisation and address when known.
Subject Short statement describing the purpose of the letter.
Salutation Dear Sir/Madam or an appropriate named greeting.
Opening Paragraph State the position and source of the vacancy, if relevant.
Middle Paragraph Highlight relevant education, skills, experience or achievements.
Closing Paragraph Express interest in an interview and thank the reader.
Closing Yours sincerely / Yours faithfully, followed by name.
Enclosure Mention the attached CV/resume when appropriate.

Exam Tip: In an exam, follow the format given by your syllabus/institution and use a formal tone. Keep the letter focused rather than copying the complete resume into it.

8. Resume

Definition

A resume is a concise document that summarises a candidate's qualifications, relevant skills, education, experience, projects and achievements for a particular opportunity.

The main purpose of a resume is to communicate the candidate's most relevant information quickly and encourage the employer to consider the applicant for the next stage of the selection process.

Features of a Good Resume

  • Clear and easy to scan.
  • Relevant to the position.
  • Accurate and up to date.
  • Professionally formatted.
  • Uses action-oriented descriptions where appropriate.
  • Free from spelling and grammatical errors.
  • Focuses on skills, achievements and relevant experience.

9. Types of Resume

Type Main Feature Useful When
Chronological Resume Emphasises work history in a time-based order, commonly with recent experience first. You have a stable and relevant career history.
Functional Resume Emphasises skills, competencies and achievements rather than employment chronology. You want to highlight transferable skills or have career gaps.
Combination Resume Combines a strong skills section with a chronological experience section. You have both relevant skills and useful experience.
Targeted Resume Specifically tailored to a particular job description or role. You want to match your profile closely with a specific vacancy.

10. Important Components of a Resume

Contact Information

Name, phone number, professional email and relevant professional links.

Career Objective/Summary

Short statement describing your professional direction and value.

Education

Relevant qualifications, institution and dates.

Skills

Technical and soft skills relevant to the role.

Experience

Relevant employment, internship or practical experience.

Projects

11. Difference Between CV and Resume

Basis CV Resume
Meaning Curriculum Vitae; a detailed record of academic and professional background. A concise summary of qualifications and relevant experience.
Length May be longer when detailed academic/professional information is required. Usually shorter and focused on relevant information.
Focus Broad and comprehensive. Targeted toward a specific opportunity.
Content May include detailed education, research, publications, experience, achievements and other sections. Usually emphasises relevant skills, experience, education, projects and achievements.
Customisation May change less frequently, although it should still be relevant and updated. Commonly tailored for each job or role.
Common Use Academic, research and professional applications where detailed background is useful. Job applications and many professional recruitment situations.
Important clarification: The terms “CV” and “resume” are not used identically everywhere. In some countries they are treated as nearly interchangeable. For exams, use the distinction expected by your course while remembering that actual employer practices vary by region and organisation.

12. Common Mistakes in CV and Resume

  1. Spelling and grammatical errors.
  2. Using an unprofessional email address.
  3. Adding irrelevant personal information.
  4. Making false or exaggerated claims.
  5. Using the same generic resume for every role without tailoring relevant skills.
  6. Writing very long descriptions instead of concise points.
  7. Using inconsistent formatting, fonts or headings.
  8. Including outdated or incorrect contact details.
  9. Listing skills without evidence, projects or experience where evidence can reasonably be provided.
  10. Ignoring the job description and required skills.
Professional Rule: A good CV/resume should be truthful, relevant, readable and easy for the employer to scan.

14. Quick Revision

CV: Detailed record of academic/professional background.

Resume: Concise, relevant summary prepared for an opportunity.

Covering Letter: Formal letter introducing the applicant and explaining the application.

CV Types: Chronological, Functional, Combination, Academic.

Resume Types: Chronological, Functional, Combination, Targeted.

CV vs Resume: CV is generally more comprehensive; resume is generally more concise and targeted, although usage varies by country.

Exam Mantra: Remember the three documents as: CV = detailed profile → Resume = targeted summary → Covering Letter = formal introduction/application message.
