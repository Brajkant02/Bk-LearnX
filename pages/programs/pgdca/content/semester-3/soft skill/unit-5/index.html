Interview Techniques & Interview Preparation

Detailed, exam-oriented notes for effective interview preparation and professional communication.

1. Introduction to Interview

An interview is a formal or semi-formal conversation between an interviewer and a candidate. It is conducted to collect information about the candidate's education, skills, experience, personality, communication ability, suitability and interest in a particular job, internship, course or opportunity.

Purpose of an Interview

  • To assess whether a candidate is suitable for a particular role.
  • To understand the candidate's qualifications and practical skills.
  • To evaluate communication and interpersonal skills.
  • To understand problem-solving and decision-making abilities.
  • To assess confidence, attitude and professional behaviour.
  • To give the candidate an opportunity to learn about the organisation and role.

Knowledge

The interviewer may assess subject knowledge, technical concepts and awareness of the role.

Skills

Practical, technical, communication and problem-solving skills may be evaluated.

Attitude

Professionalism, willingness to learn, responsibility and adaptability are important.

Communication

Clear speech, listening, appropriate vocabulary and confident expression matter.

2. Preparing for an Interview

Interview preparation is the process of getting ready for the questions, responsibilities, environment and communication requirements of an interview. Good preparation improves confidence and helps the candidate present relevant information clearly.

Major Areas of Preparation

  1. Understand the role: Read the job description and identify the main responsibilities and required skills.
  2. Research the organisation: Learn about its products, services, work, values and general background from reliable sources.
  3. Review your resume: Be ready to explain every important qualification, project, skill and experience mentioned in it.
  4. Prepare common questions: Practise answers to questions about yourself, education, skills, strengths, weaknesses, goals and experience.
  5. Revise technical knowledge: Review concepts relevant to the position.
  6. Prepare examples: Think of real examples that demonstrate teamwork, problem-solving, leadership or learning.
  7. Practise communication: Speak clearly and avoid unnecessary fillers.
  8. Prepare questions: Keep a few thoughtful questions ready for the interviewer.
  9. Plan logistics: Confirm time, location, internet connection, device or other requirements.
Key idea: Preparation is not memorising every sentence. It is understanding your own profile and preparing clear points that can be adapted to different questions.

Location Know the route and expected travel time for an in-person interview.

Mindset Stay calm, positive and ready to listen carefully.

4. During the Interview

Professional Behaviour

  • Arrive on time or join an online interview a few minutes early.
  • Greet the interviewer politely.
  • Listen carefully before answering.
  • Maintain appropriate eye contact in an in-person interview.
  • Sit with an attentive and professional posture.
  • Speak clearly and at a comfortable pace.
  • Give relevant answers instead of unnecessarily long explanations.
  • If you do not know something, be honest rather than inventing an answer.
  • Ask for clarification if a question is unclear.
  • Thank the interviewer at the end.
Remember: Confidence does not mean speaking continuously. A confident candidate listens, thinks, and then gives a clear answer.

5. After the Interview

  • Review the questions you were asked.
  • Note areas where your answer could be improved.
  • Record useful feedback from a mock or real interview.
  • Complete any requested follow-up task or document.
  • If appropriate, send a brief professional thank-you message.
  • Continue preparing for the next stage instead of assuming the process is complete.

6. Interview Formats

An interview can be conducted in different formats depending on the organisation, purpose, number of interviewers and location.

Format Description Important Preparation Point
One-to-One Interview One interviewer interacts directly with one candidate. Focus on clear communication and detailed answers.
Panel Interview Multiple interviewers ask questions to one candidate. Address the person asking the question while remaining aware of the whole panel.
Group Interview Multiple candidates may participate together. Show communication, teamwork and respectful participation.
Face-to-Face Interview Interview takes place in person. Prepare professional appearance, documents and travel arrangements.
Telephone Interview Interview is conducted through a phone call. Focus strongly on voice clarity, listening and concise answers.
Video/Online Interview Interview is conducted using a video-conferencing platform. Test technology, camera, microphone, background and internet connection.
Structured Interview Interviewers use a planned set of questions or evaluation criteria. Prepare systematically for common competency areas.
Unstructured Interview Conversation is more flexible and questions may change according to the discussion. Know your profile well and be ready for follow-up questions.

7. Types of Interview Questions

1. General Questions

These questions help the interviewer understand the candidate's background and motivation.

Examples: Tell me about yourself. Why are you interested in this role? What are your career goals?

2. Educational Questions

These questions focus on academic background, subjects, coursework and learning.

Examples: Why did you choose your course? Which subject do you enjoy most? What have you learned from your academic projects?

3. Technical Questions

Technical questions check knowledge and practical understanding related to the position.

Examples: Explain a programming concept. How would you troubleshoot a technical problem? What tools have you used in your projects?

4. Behavioural Questions

Behavioural questions ask about past situations to understand how a candidate behaves in real circumstances.

Examples: Tell me about a time when you worked in a team. Describe a difficult problem you solved. Tell me about a mistake and what you learned from it.

5. Situational Questions

These questions describe a hypothetical situation and ask what you would do.

Examples: What would you do if you had a tight deadline? How would you handle disagreement with a teammate?

6. Strengths and Weaknesses Questions

These questions explore self-awareness and professional development.

Example: What is one of your strengths? What skill are you currently working to improve?

7. Motivation Questions

These questions explore why the candidate wants the role, organisation or field.

Examples: Why do you want to join us? Why did you choose this career? What motivates you to learn?

8. Problem-Solving Questions

These questions assess reasoning, analysis and decision-making.

Examples: How would you approach an unfamiliar problem? What steps would you take before deciding on a solution?

8. How to Answer Interview Questions

“Tell Me About Yourself”

A useful answer can follow this order:

  1. Present status or educational/professional background.
  2. Relevant skills.
  3. Important project, experience or achievement.
  4. Why the opportunity is relevant to your goals.

Sample Answer: “I am a PGDCA student with an interest in web development and software technologies. I have been building my skills in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Python and SQL, and I have worked on academic and personal projects to apply these skills. I enjoy learning through practical work and problem-solving. I am now looking for an opportunity where I can strengthen my technical skills and contribute to real projects.”

Behavioural Questions – STAR Method

The STAR method can help organise answers to many behavioural questions.

Step Meaning What to Explain
S – Situation Background What was happening?
T – Task Responsibility What did you need to achieve?
A – Action Your response What exactly did you do?
R – Result Outcome What happened or what did you learn?
Tip: Use real examples whenever possible. Focus on your own contribution instead of only describing what the whole team did.

9. Mock Interviews

Definition

A mock interview is a practice interview designed to simulate the conditions of an actual interview. It may be conducted by a teacher, trainer, mentor, friend, career counsellor or another person acting as the interviewer.

Purpose of Mock Interviews

  • Provide realistic interview practice.
  • Help candidates become familiar with common interview questions.
  • Improve speaking and listening skills.
  • Build confidence.
  • Identify weaknesses before the actual interview.
  • Provide an opportunity to receive feedback.

Typical Mock Interview Process

  1. Select a job role or interview scenario.
  2. Prepare a resume and relevant job description.
  3. Assign an interviewer.
  4. Conduct the interview under realistic conditions.
  5. Ask general, technical, behavioural and situational questions as appropriate.
  6. Observe communication, body language and answer quality.
  7. Give constructive feedback.
  8. Identify improvement areas.
  9. Repeat the exercise after making improvements.

10. Benefits of Mock Interviews

Benefit Explanation
Builds Confidence Repeated practice can make the interview environment feel more familiar.
Reduces Nervousness Candidates learn how to manage pressure and organise their thoughts.
Improves Communication Practice helps improve clarity, speaking pace, vocabulary and listening.
Identifies Weaknesses Feedback can reveal unclear answers, filler words, poor posture or gaps in preparation.
Improves Answer Quality Candidates learn to give concise, relevant and structured answers.
Improves Body Language Practice can help candidates become more aware of eye contact, posture and gestures.
Improves Time Management Candidates learn to answer within a reasonable amount of time.
Provides Feedback An observer can identify strengths and suggest specific improvements.
Improves Technical Readiness Role-specific questions can expose technical topics that need revision.
Increases Familiarity The candidate becomes more comfortable with the overall interview process.
Best Practice: A mock interview is most useful when it is followed by specific feedback and another practice round. Practice without reflection is less effective.

11. Interview Do's and Don'ts

✓ Do's

  • Be punctual.
  • Dress appropriately.
  • Listen carefully.
  • Answer honestly.
  • Use clear language.
  • Support claims with examples.
  • Show willingness to learn.
  • Maintain professional body language.
  • Ask relevant questions when invited.

✗ Don'ts

  • Do not arrive unnecessarily late.
  • Do not interrupt repeatedly.
  • Do not invent qualifications or experience.
  • Do not speak negatively about previous teachers, employers or colleagues.
  • Do not give extremely long unrelated answers.
  • Do not use excessive slang.
  • Do not ignore technical preparation.
  • Do not use your phone unnecessarily during the interview.
  • Do not panic if you need a moment to think.

14. Quick Revision

Interview: A conversation used to assess a candidate's suitability, knowledge, skills and professional qualities.

Preparation: Research role → research organisation → review resume → practise questions → revise skills → prepare logistics.

Formats: One-to-one, panel, group, face-to-face, telephone, online, structured and unstructured.

Question Types: General, educational, technical, behavioural, situational, strengths/weaknesses, motivation and problem-solving.

STAR: Situation → Task → Action → Result.

Mock Interview: Practice interview that simulates an actual interview.

Benefits: Confidence, communication, feedback, reduced nervousness, better answers, improved body language and better preparation.

Exam Mantra: Prepare your profile, understand the role, practise realistic questions, communicate clearly, stay honest and use feedback to improve.
