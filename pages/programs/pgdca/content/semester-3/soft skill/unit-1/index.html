Basics of Communication

Definition and Process of Communication

Communication

Definition: Communication is the process which connects people by enabling them to share information, whether personal, professional or social. It is a process of expressing and transferring ideas, knowledge, information, feelings and thoughts through a common medium/channel.

Communication is an essential part of human life. It connects people, helps them share ideas and information, and creates understanding between the sender and receiver.

Evolution of Communication

According to the source, communication developed gradually:

During the Stone Age, body language and sounds were used for communication. Later, signs, symbols, language, speech and writing developed. Modern technology provided communication tools such as radio, TV, telephone, mobile phones, computers, the internet, radar and satellites.

Interesting point: According to the source, communication is not limited to humans; animals also use sounds, expressions and behaviour to share warnings, information about food and water, and group information.

Process of Communication

Effective communication ke liye different skills required hoti hain:

  1. Intelligence (subject competence)
  2. Language skill
  3. Organizational competence — systematic and structured presentation
  4. Awareness about channels and coordination
  5. Updated knowledge of transmission devices and methods

Essential Factors

Factor Meaning
Sender The source of the message. The sender develops the message using knowledge and skills.
Receiver The end point of communication that receives and decodes the message and then provides feedback.
Message The information or idea created by the sender and transmitted to the receiver.
Channel The medium through which the message is passed or transmitted.

Communication is not a passive or random process; the source describes it as a two-dimensional, dynamic and interactive process.

Five Steps of Communication

1. Ideation

Ideation is the first step of communication. The sender forms or selects an idea to be transmitted as a message. The sender's subject competence—knowledge, experience and thinking ability—is important for this step. Poor ideation can make communication ineffective from the very beginning.

2. Encoding

Encoding means changing an idea into coded language. The language may be verbal or non-verbal. In this step, the type, style, tone and length of the message are decided. Linguistic and organizational competence are important.

Exam Point: Source ke अनुसार message transmit hone ke baad sender usse change ya stop nahi kar sakta.

3. Transmission

Transmission means sending the message from the sender to the receiver. The sender selects a suitable method from the available channels. Incorrect channel selection can break or distort communication.

Channels shown in source: Face-to-Face, Mobile, Electronic, Written and Broadcast.

4. Decoding

Decoding is the step where the receiver's involvement begins. The receiver analyses the received message and understands its meaning.

For effective decoding, a similar or common medium should be available between the sender and receiver, especially language and environment. Example: A person who understands Hindi cannot decode a Tamil message if Tamil is not known to that person.

5. Response / Feedback

Response or feedback is the last step of the communication process. It represents the receiver's reply or response to the message. Feedback depends on the receiver's decoding.

Key Idea: If the receiver gives the desired response, communication can be considered effective; otherwise, its effectiveness may be reduced.

Types of Communication

The style, tone, word selection, length and delivery method of communication may depend on the situation, the receiver's status and the urgency of the message. Therefore, different communication methods are used for different situations.

Four Bases of Classification

  1. Style and purpose: Formal and Informal
  2. Language, media, method, expression: Verbal and Non-Verbal
  3. Method of delivery: Oral and Written
  4. Flow in organization hierarchy: Vertical (Upward/Downward), Horizontal and Diagonal/Crosswise

Verbal Communication

Meaning: Verbal communication means expressing feelings, knowledge, ideas and information by using words or language.

Non-Verbal Communication

Meaning: Words/language ke bina communication karna non-verbal communication hai. Isme face expressions, body language, gestures, symbols etc. use hote hain.

Non-verbal communication has been important since primitive times. When words are limited, facial expressions, postures, gestures, voice dynamics, space and symbols help convey meaning.

2. Dressing Sense / Appearance

Dress selection personality, standard and taste ke baare mein message deti hai. Dressing occasion ke purpose ke according honi chahiye. Isme jewellery, make-up, shoes, hairstyle etc. bhi included ho sakte hain.

Speech verbal communication se related hai, while voice dynamics non-verbal communication ka part hai. Voice dynamics mein voice quality, rate, pitch, volume, speaking style, rhythm, intonation and stress included hain.

  • It reduces a monotonous tone.

4. Proxemics (Space Around You Language)

Proxemics is the study of spatial distance or physical space between the speaker and listener. It explains the use of physical space in interpersonal relations and in different cultures and situations.

6. Signs and Symbols

Signs and symbols are primitive but useful forms of non-verbal communication. They provide clues for particular statements and reduce the need for words.

  • They easily grab attention.
  • They can communicate more strongly than words or sentences.
  • They convey a message or information using less space.
  • They save time and provide quick expression.
  • They are easily noticeable to passersby.
  • Language barriers avoid karte hain.
  • Universal nature ho sakti hai.

Flow Based Communication

In the professional field, people at different posts communicate for coordination and smooth functioning within an organization.

HorizontalVertical → Upward / DownwardDiagonal / Crosswise

1. Horizontal Communication

When two people at the same status or hierarchy level discuss something to reach a conclusion or communicate with each other in an organization, it is called horizontal communication.

2. Vertical Communication

Communication between people at different hierarchy positions is called vertical communication. It has two types:

  • Vertically Downward: A person at a higher hierarchy level communicates with a person at a lower hierarchy level. Example: Engineer → Junior Engineer.
  • Vertically Upward: A person at a lower hierarchy level communicates with a person at a higher hierarchy level. Example: Junior Engineer → Engineer.

3. Diagonal / Cross Communication

When communication does not follow hierarchy-level rules and people at different levels communicate directly, it is called diagonal or cross communication. Example: The boss of a company directly calls an office clerk without informing the head of the clerical department.

Informal Communication

Informal communication is personal communication or communication without formality. It is used with relatives, friends, neighbours and well-known people in a personal style. It helps develop relationships and strong bonds and, according to the source, can help release stress and tension in family, society and the workplace.

Formal Communication

Formal communication is impersonal communication or communication with formality. It is used with people related to jobs or professions for professional purposes. It helps in the smooth functioning of an organization and fills the gap between departments. It is also called professional communication.
