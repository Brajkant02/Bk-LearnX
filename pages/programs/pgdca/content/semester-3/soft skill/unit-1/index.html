Basics of Communication

Definition and Process of Communication

Communication

Definition: Communication is the process which connects people by enabling them to share information, whether personal, professional or social. It is a process of expressing and transferring ideas, knowledge, information, feelings and thoughts through a common medium/channel.

Communication is an essential part of human life. It connects people, helps them share ideas and information, and creates understanding between the sender and receiver.

Evolution of Communication

According to the source, communication developed gradually:

During the Stone Age, body language and sounds were used for communication. Later, signs, symbols, language, speech and writing developed. Modern technology provided communication tools such as radio, TV, telephone, mobile phones, computers, the internet, radar and satellites.

Interesting point: According to the source, communication is not limited to humans; animals also use sounds, expressions and behaviour to share warnings, information about food and water, and group information.

Process of Communication

Effective communication ke liye different skills required hoti hain:

  1. Intelligence (subject competence)
  2. Language skill
  3. Organizational competence — systematic and structured presentation
  4. Awareness about channels and coordination
  5. Updated knowledge of transmission devices and methods

Essential Factors

Factor Meaning
Sender The source of the message. The sender develops the message using knowledge and skills.
Receiver The end point of communication that receives and decodes the message and then provides feedback.
Message The information or idea created by the sender and transmitted to the receiver.
Channel The medium through which the message is passed or transmitted.

Communication is not a passive or random process; the source describes it as a two-dimensional, dynamic and interactive process.

Five Steps of Communication

1. Ideation

Ideation is the first step of communication. The sender forms or selects an idea to be transmitted as a message. The sender's subject competence—knowledge, experience and thinking ability—is important for this step. Poor ideation can make communication ineffective from the very beginning.

2. Encoding

Encoding means changing an idea into coded language. The language may be verbal or non-verbal. In this step, the type, style, tone and length of the message are decided. Linguistic and organizational competence are important.

Exam Point: Source ke अनुसार message transmit hone ke baad sender usse change ya stop nahi kar sakta.

3. Transmission

Transmission means sending the message from the sender to the receiver. The sender selects a suitable method from the available channels. Incorrect channel selection can break or distort communication.

Channels shown in source: Face-to-Face, Mobile, Electronic, Written and Broadcast.

4. Decoding

Decoding is the step where the receiver's involvement begins. The receiver analyses the received message and understands its meaning.

For effective decoding, a similar or common medium should be available between the sender and receiver, especially language and environment. Example: A person who understands Hindi cannot decode a Tamil message if Tamil is not known to that person.

5. Response / Feedback

Response or feedback is the last step of the communication process. It represents the receiver's reply or response to the message. Feedback depends on the receiver's decoding.

Key Idea: If the receiver gives the desired response, communication can be considered effective; otherwise, its effectiveness may be reduced.
