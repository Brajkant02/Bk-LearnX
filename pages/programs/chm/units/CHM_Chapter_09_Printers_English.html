Printers

Introduction

A printer is an output device that produces a hard copy of information stored in a computer. It converts digital documents, text, images, and graphics into a printed form on paper or another suitable material.

Printers are commonly used in homes, schools, offices, shops, banks, and industries. Different printers use different technologies to form images and transfer them onto paper. Common types include Dot Matrix Printers, Inkjet Printers, Laser Printers, and Multifunction Printers.

Image Formation Method

Image formation is the process by which a printer converts digital information into text, pictures, and graphics on paper. The computer sends print data to the printer. The printer processes this data and forms the required image using its printing technology.

1. Impact Printing

In impact printing, a print head or printing element physically strikes an inked ribbon against the paper. The impact transfers ink onto the paper to form characters or images. Dot Matrix Printers are a common example of impact printers.

  • Uses physical contact between the printing element, ribbon, and paper.
  • Can print on multi-part or carbon-copy forms.
  • Usually produces more noise than non-impact printers.

2. Non-Impact Printing

In non-impact printing, the printer forms images without striking the paper with a printing head. It may use ink droplets, laser light, toner, heat, or other methods. Inkjet and Laser Printers are common examples.

  • Does not use a striking print head to form characters on paper.
  • Generally operates more quietly than impact printers.
  • Can produce high-quality text, graphics, and photographs, depending on the printer.

Difference Between Impact and Non-Impact Printing

Basis Impact Printing Non-Impact Printing
Printing Method Print element strikes an inked ribbon against paper. Forms images without striking the paper.
Noise Usually noisier. Usually quieter.
Examples Dot Matrix Printer. Inkjet Printer and Laser Printer.
Multi-Part Forms Can print on suitable multi-part forms. Generally cannot print through carbon-copy multi-part forms in the same way.

Printing Mechanism

A printing mechanism is the method and set of components used by a printer to place text and images onto paper. The mechanism differs according to the printer type.

Basic Printing Process

  1. Receiving Data: The printer receives the document data from a computer or another connected device.
  2. Processing: The printer controller or print system interprets the data and prepares the page layout.
  3. Image Formation: The printer creates the required text or image using pins, ink droplets, or toner, depending on its technology.
  4. Transfer to Paper: The printing mechanism places ink or toner onto the paper.
  5. Output: The printed sheet is moved out of the printer.

For example, a Dot Matrix Printer forms characters by striking pins against an ink ribbon. An Inkjet Printer sprays tiny ink droplets onto paper. A Laser Printer uses a laser-based imaging process and toner powder to create the printed page.

Dot Matrix Printer (DMP)

A Dot Matrix Printer (DMP) is an impact printer that forms characters and images using a pattern of small dots. It contains a print head with a set of tiny pins. These pins strike an inked ribbon against the paper to create printed dots.

Main Parts of a Dot Matrix Printer

  • Print Head: Contains small pins that move forward to strike the ribbon.
  • Ink Ribbon: Transfers ink onto the paper when struck by the pins.
  • Platen: A rubber roller that supports and moves the paper during printing.
  • Paper Feed Mechanism: Moves paper through the printer. Some models use tractor-feed mechanisms for continuous paper.
  • Control Circuit: Controls the print head, pin movement, and paper feed.

Image Placeholder: Dot Matrix Printer

Working of a Dot Matrix Printer

  1. The computer sends text or image data to the printer.
  2. The printer controller converts the data into dot patterns.
  3. The print head moves across the paper.
  4. Selected pins strike the ink ribbon at specific positions.
  5. The ribbon transfers ink dots onto the paper to form characters or images.
  6. The paper feed mechanism moves the paper to the next line.

Advantages of DMP

  • Can print on suitable multi-part forms and continuous stationery.
  • Uses relatively low-cost ribbon consumables.
  • Useful for invoices, receipts, and forms in environments where impact printing is required.
  • Can be suitable for continuous operation in certain business applications.

Limitations of DMP

  • Produces more noise than most non-impact printers.
  • Usually has lower print quality for graphics and photographs.
  • Generally prints more slowly than modern Inkjet and Laser Printers.
  • Print quality may become faint when the ribbon wears out.

Inkjet Printer

An Inkjet Printer is a non-impact printer that creates text and images by spraying very small droplets of liquid ink onto paper through tiny nozzles in the print head. It is commonly used for home printing, school work, documents, and colour photographs.

Main Parts of an Inkjet Printer

  • Print Head: Contains tiny nozzles that release ink droplets onto the paper.
  • Ink Cartridges or Ink Tanks: Store the liquid ink used for printing.
  • Carriage: Holds and moves the print head across the paper in many printer designs.
  • Paper Feed Rollers: Pull paper into the printer and move it during printing.
  • Control Circuit: Controls printing operations and communication with the computer.

Image Placeholder: Inkjet Printer

Working of an Inkjet Printer

  1. The computer sends the document or image to the printer.
  2. The printer processes the data and determines where each ink droplet should be placed.
  3. The print head moves across the paper.
  4. Tiny nozzles spray controlled droplets of ink onto the required positions.
  5. The paper feed mechanism moves the paper so that the next line or section can be printed.
  6. The ink dries on the paper, producing the final text or image.

Advantages of Inkjet Printer

  • Can produce good-quality colour images and photographs.
  • Usually has a lower initial purchase cost than many office laser printers.
  • Available in compact models for home and personal use.
  • Can print on different types of supported paper and photo media.

Limitations of Inkjet Printer

  • Ink cartridges or refill ink may increase running costs.
  • Print heads and nozzles may become clogged if the printer is unused for a long time.
  • Printing speed may be lower than that of many laser printers for large document jobs.
  • Some printed pages may need time to dry, depending on the ink and paper.

Laser Printer

A Laser Printer is a non-impact printer that uses a laser-based imaging system and powdered toner to print text and images. It is widely used in offices, schools, and businesses because it can produce sharp text and print many pages efficiently.

Main Parts of a Laser Printer

  • Toner Cartridge: Contains fine powdered toner used to form text and images.
  • Photosensitive Drum: Holds an electrical charge pattern that represents the page image.
  • Laser or LED Imaging Unit: Creates the image pattern on the photosensitive drum.
  • Developer Unit: Helps toner attach to the image areas on the drum.
  • Transfer Roller: Helps transfer toner from the drum onto the paper.
  • Fuser Unit: Uses heat and pressure to bond toner permanently to the paper.
  • Paper Feed System: Moves paper through the printer.

Image Placeholder: Main Parts of a Laser Printer

Working of a Laser Printer

  1. Charging: The photosensitive drum receives a uniform electrical charge.
  2. Exposing: A laser beam or imaging unit creates an electrostatic image pattern on the drum.
  3. Developing: Toner particles attach to the image areas on the drum.
  4. Transferring: The toner image is transferred from the drum onto the paper.
  5. Fusing: The paper passes through heated rollers that apply heat and pressure to bond toner to the paper.
  6. Cleaning and Resetting: Remaining toner is removed or managed, and the drum is prepared for the next page.

Advantages of Laser Printer

  • Usually prints text quickly and produces sharp characters.
  • Suitable for high-volume document printing.
  • Toner does not dry out in the same way as liquid ink.
  • Often provides a low cost per page for large print volumes, depending on the model and consumables.

Limitations of Laser Printer

  • Initial purchase cost may be higher than that of basic Inkjet Printers.
  • Toner cartridges and imaging components need replacement when their service life ends.
  • Colour laser models may be more expensive than monochrome models.
  • The fuser unit becomes hot during operation and requires proper handling during maintenance.

Multifunction Printer (MFP)

A Multifunction Printer (MFP), also called an All-in-One Printer, combines multiple office functions in a single device. Depending on the model, it can print, scan, copy, and sometimes send or receive faxes.

Common Functions

  • Printing: Produces hard copies of documents, photographs, and other files.
  • Scanning: Converts paper documents and photographs into digital files.
  • Copying: Creates paper copies of documents without requiring a separate computer for every copy operation.
  • Faxing: Some models can send and receive fax documents.
  • Connectivity: Many models support USB, Wi-Fi, Ethernet, or mobile printing, depending on their specifications.

Image Placeholder: Multifunction Printer

Working of a Multifunction Printer

The working process depends on the function selected. For printing, it uses an Inkjet or Laser printing mechanism. For scanning, a light source and image sensor capture the document and convert it into digital data. For copying, the device scans the original document and sends the captured image to its printing system.

Advantages of MFP

  • Combines several functions in one device.
  • Saves desk space compared with separate devices.
  • Can reduce the need to purchase and maintain multiple machines.
  • Useful for homes, schools, and small or medium-sized offices.

How a Printer Works

Although different printers use different printing technologies, the overall process begins when a user gives a print command from a computer or another device. The print system prepares the document, sends the data to the printer, and the printer forms the page using its own mechanism.

General Steps

  1. Print Command: The user selects the Print option in an application.
  2. Print Settings: The user selects options such as paper size, page range, orientation, colour mode, and number of copies.
  3. Data Processing: The operating system and printer driver prepare the document data in a format the printer can process.
  4. Data Transfer: The prepared data is sent through a connection such as USB, Wi-Fi, or Ethernet.
  5. Image Formation: The printer uses its printing mechanism to form the required text and images.
  6. Paper Movement: The paper feed mechanism moves the sheet through the printer.
  7. Printed Output: The finished page is delivered to the output tray.

Printer Troubleshooting

Printer troubleshooting is the process of identifying and solving problems that prevent a printer from working correctly. Problems may be related to power, connectivity, paper feeding, ink or toner, drivers, print settings, or internal printer components.

Common Printer Problems and Solutions

Problem Possible Cause Basic Solution
Printer does not turn on Power cable is loose, power outlet is faulty, or the printer has a hardware problem. Check the power cable and outlet. Ensure the power switch is on. If it still does not start, contact a service technician.
Printer is not detected USB or network connection problem, incorrect settings, or driver issue. Check the cable or network connection, restart the printer and computer, and verify the correct printer is selected.
Paper jam Paper is incorrectly loaded, damaged, damp, or stuck inside the feed path. Follow the printer's instructions to remove jammed paper gently. Do not pull paper against the direction recommended by the manufacturer.
Blank pages are printed Empty ink or toner, blocked inkjet nozzles, incorrectly installed cartridge, or print settings issue. Check ink or toner levels, install cartridges correctly, and run the supported print-head cleaning or test procedure.
Faded or light printing Low ink or toner, worn ribbon, blocked nozzles, or print density settings. Check consumables, replace a worn ribbon or empty cartridge, and verify print quality settings.
Lines or spots on printouts Dirty print head, damaged drum, toner contamination, or dirty printer components. Use the manufacturer's cleaning procedure and inspect or replace the relevant consumable if required.
Printer prints slowly High-quality print mode, large files, low system resources, or connection problems. Check print settings, close unnecessary applications, and verify the connection. Select a suitable print quality for the task.
Incorrect colours Low or empty colour ink, blocked nozzles, incorrect colour settings, or unsuitable paper. Check colour levels, run a nozzle check or cleaning cycle if supported, and verify colour and paper settings.
Print job remains in queue Print spooler issue, communication interruption, or printer error. Check the printer status, cancel the stuck job if safe, and restart the printer and print service according to the operating system instructions.

Basic Troubleshooting Procedure

  1. Check whether the printer is switched on and has no warning light or error message.
  2. Check the power cable and USB or network connection.
  3. Confirm that paper is loaded correctly and that there is no paper jam.
  4. Check ink, toner, or ribbon levels according to the printer type.
  5. Verify that the correct printer is selected and that the print queue is not paused.
  6. Check the printer driver and install the correct driver if required.
  7. Print a test page to check whether the problem has been resolved.
  8. If the problem continues, consult the printer manual or an authorized service technician.
