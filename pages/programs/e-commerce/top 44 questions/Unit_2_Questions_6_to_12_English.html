Q6. What are the Different Modes of Electronic Payment?

Electronic Payment

Electronic Payment Electronic Payment, or E-Payment, is the process of paying for Goods and Services through Electronic means instead of Cash or Physical Currency. It uses the Internet, Mobile Applications, Banking Networks, Cards and Digital Payment Systems. Electronic Payment is also called Digital Payment.

Example: When a Customer pays for a product on an Online Shopping Website using UPI, a Debit Card or Net Banking, it is called Electronic Payment.

Different Modes of Electronic Payment

1. Credit Card

A Credit Card is a Payment Card that allows a Customer to make purchases by borrowing from a Bank or Card Issuer within a specified Credit Limit. The Customer must pay the Bill within the prescribed time later. During an Online Transaction, the Card Number, Expiry Date and required Authentication are entered.

Example: Making a Payment with a Credit Card while purchasing a Laptop from a Website.

2. Debit Card

A Debit Card is directly linked to the Customer's Bank Account. When a Payment is made, the amount is generally deducted from that Account. A Debit Card is used at ATMs, POS Machines in shops and for Online Shopping.

3. UPI (Unified Payments Interface)

UPI is an Instant Payment System developed in India. It allows Users to link their Bank Accounts to a UPI-enabled Application and send or receive money through a Mobile Phone. A UPI ID, Mobile Number or QR Code can be used. The Transaction is authorized using a UPI PIN.

Example: Making a Payment through UPI by scanning a QR Code at a shop.

4. Internet Banking / Net Banking

In Internet Banking, a Customer performs Fund Transfers, Bill Payments and other Banking Transactions through the Bank's Official Website or Internet Banking Portal. Login Credentials and Additional Authentication may be required.

5. Mobile Banking

Mobile Banking allows Customers to use Banking Services through a Bank's Mobile Application. A User can perform tasks such as Balance Checks, Fund Transfers, Recharges and Bill Payments.

6. Mobile Wallets

A Mobile Wallet is a Digital Application or Service through which a User can securely use funds or Payment Instruments for making Payments. Some Wallets allow users to Add Balance, while others make Payments through a Linked Bank Account or Card.

Example: Making a Mobile Recharge or Merchant Payment through a supported Wallet App.

7. NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer)

NEFT is a Bank-to-Bank Electronic Fund Transfer System. It allows money to be transferred from one Bank Account to another. It is used for Fund Transfers within the country, and Transactions are Processed in scheduled Settlement Batches.

8. RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement)

RTGS is mainly used for transferring large amounts of money. In this system, each Transaction is Settled in Real Time on an Individual Basis. It is useful for large and time-sensitive Payments.

9. IMPS (Immediate Payment Service)

IMPS is an Instant Interbank Electronic Fund Transfer Service. Depending on the available Channels, it allows money to be sent almost instantly. It is useful for Mobile and Internet Banking.

10. Electronic Cheque (E-Cheque)

An E-Cheque is the Electronic form of a traditional Cheque. In this method, a Payment Instruction is issued in Digital form and Electronic Authentication may be used.

11. QR Code Payment

In QR Code Payment, the Merchant's QR Code is scanned to obtain Payment Details. The Customer then confirms the amount in the Payment App and authorizes the Transaction. It is very useful in shops and small Businesses.

Advantages of Electronic Payment

  • Fast Transactions:
    Many Digital Payments are completed within a short time.
  • Convenience:
    A Customer can make a Payment using a Mobile Phone while sitting at home or while visiting a shop.
  • Less Cash Handling:
    The need to carry and count Cash is reduced.
  • Transaction Records:
    Digital History makes it easy to view Payment Records.
  • Online Shopping Support:
    Payments can be made instantly on E-Commerce Websites.
  • Wider Availability:
    Many Payment Methods are available across different Banks and Merchants.

Limitations and Safety

Electronic Payments may involve risks such as Internet or Network Failure, Technical Errors, Fraud, Phishing and Unauthorized Transactions. Users should not Share their OTP, PIN or Password with anyone. They should use only Official Applications and Secure Websites and Verify the Receiver and Amount before making a Payment.

Q7. What are the Major Threats to E-Commerce Payment Systems?

E-Commerce Payment System

E-Commerce Payment System An E-Commerce Payment System is a Digital arrangement through which an Online Buyer pays for a Product or Service purchased on a Website or Application. Payment Information and Transaction Data are exchanged among the Customer, Merchant, Bank, Payment Gateway and Payment Network. Activities and weaknesses that harm the Security of this System are called Payment System Threats.

Cybercriminals target Users, Merchants and Payment Platforms through different techniques. Therefore, it is necessary to understand these Threats and adopt appropriate Security Measures.

Example: If a Fraudster creates a Fake Shopping Website, steals a Customer's Card Details and misuses them, it is a Cyber Threat to the E-Commerce Payment System.

Major Threats to E-Commerce Payment Systems

1. Phishing Attacks

In Phishing, Fraudsters deceive Users through Fake Emails, SMS messages, Websites or other Messages. They send messages that look like they come from a Bank or Payment Company and attempt to obtain Login Details, OTPs or Card Information.

Example: A Customer receives a Fake Message stating that their Bank Account will be Blocked and that they must enter their Details by visiting the provided Link.

2. Identity Theft

Identity Theft occurs when someone's Personal or Financial Information is stolen and used in their name to carry out Unauthorized Transactions or other wrongful activities. This may involve misuse of their Name, Address, Account Details and other identity-related information.

3. Credit Card and Debit Card Fraud

In this type of Fraud, stolen Card Details are used to make Payments without the Cardholder's permission. Card Information may be obtained through a Data Breach, Phishing or insecure Websites.

4. Malware and Spyware

Malware is Malicious Software that can harm a Computer or Mobile Phone. Spyware can monitor a User's activities and sensitive information. Malware such as a Keylogger can record information typed on a Keyboard.

5. Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) Attack

In this Attack, an Attacker secretly attempts to Intercept or modify Communication taking place between two parties. Information sent over an insecure Network may be at risk.

6. Data Breach

A Data Breach occurs when sensitive information is Accessed or stolen without permission from an Organization's Database or System. This may include Customer Details, Passwords and Payment Information.

7. Fake Websites and Online Stores

Fraudsters create Fake E-Commerce Websites that imitate genuine Businesses. A Customer may make a Payment on such Websites but may not receive the Product, or their information may be stolen.

8. Password Attacks

Weak or Reused Passwords increase the risk of Unauthorized Access to Accounts. Attackers may attempt to Log In using techniques such as Brute Force and Credential Stuffing.

9. SQL Injection

SQL Injection is a Web Application Attack in which an Attacker sends Malicious SQL Commands through Input Fields to improperly Access or affect a Database. This can put Customer Records and other Data at risk.

10. Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)

In a DDoS Attack, a large amount of Unwanted Traffic is sent to a Website or Server. This can make the Website Slow or Unavailable and prevent Customers from completing Payments.

11. Social Engineering

In Social Engineering, an Attacker takes advantage of human behaviour rather than Technology. The Attacker may deceive a Customer or Employee into revealing an OTP, Password or Confidential Information.

12. Insider Threats

An Employee or another Authorized Person in an Organization may misuse their Access to steal or share Customer Data.

Effects of Payment System Threats

  • Financial Loss: The Customer or Merchant may suffer Financial Loss.
  • Data Theft: Personal and Financial Information may be stolen.
  • Loss of Trust: Customers may lose trust in the Website or Brand.
  • Business Disruption: The Website or Payment Services may remain unavailable for some time.
  • Legal Consequences: An Organization may face legal action for failing to comply with Data Protection and Payment regulations.

Basic Prevention Measures

Users should use Secure and Official Websites, Verify URLs, adopt Strong Passwords and Multi-Factor Authentication, and never disclose an OTP or PIN to anyone. Businesses should implement Software Updates, Encryption, Access Control, a Secure Payment Gateway, Regular Security Audits and Fraud Monitoring.

Q8. Explain Various Ways to Protect the E-Commerce Payment System.

Payment System Security

E-Commerce Payment System Security E-Commerce Payment System Security refers to the Technologies, Rules and Practices used to protect Online Transactions, Payment Details and Customer Information from Unauthorized Access, Fraud, Theft and Cyber Attacks. Its objective is to maintain the Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability of Payments.

Example: Using HTTPS, a Secure Payment Gateway, OTP Verification and Fraud Monitoring on a Shopping Website are measures for Payment System Security.

Ways to Protect E-Commerce Payment System

1. SSL/TLS Encryption and HTTPS

SSL/TLS Encryption helps protect Data sent between a Browser and a Website. HTTPS uses TLS to Encrypt Communication. This makes it difficult to read or modify Data while it is travelling across a Network. A Website should use a Valid TLS Certificate and correct Configuration.

2. Secure Payment Gateway

A Payment Gateway helps securely Process Payment Information between a Customer and a Merchant. A Business should choose a trusted Payment Provider and follow its Security Standards and Integration Guidelines. This allows Card or other Payment Transactions to be Processed systematically.

3. Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) / Multi-Factor Authentication

Authentication Verifies that the person using an Account is the correct User. Multi-Factor Authentication uses one or more Verification Factors in addition to a Password. This can make Account misuse more difficult even if the Password is stolen.

4. Strong Password Policy

Users and Employees should create Unique and Strong Passwords. A Password should have sufficient Length and different types of Characters. The same Password should not be used on multiple Websites. Using a Password Manager can also be helpful.

5. Tokenization

In Tokenization, sensitive Card Information is Replaced with a special Token. This can reduce the need to Store the actual Card Number in the Merchant's System. The Token can be used only within the relevant Payment Environment, and Controls are applied to limit its misuse.

6. Data Encryption and Secure Storage

Payment and Customer Data should be Encrypted during Storage and Transmission as required. Sensitive Information should not be Stored unnecessarily. Encryption Keys must also be Managed securely.

7. PCI DSS Compliance

PCI DSS stands for Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard. It provides a Framework of Security Requirements for protecting Cardholder Data. Businesses that accept Card Payments and their Service Providers should comply with the PCI DSS Requirements applicable to them.

8. Regular Software Updates

Websites, Servers, CMS, Plugins and Payment Software should be Updated regularly. Updates may include fixes for Security Vulnerabilities. Using old and Unsupported Software increases risk.

9. Firewall and Web Application Firewall (WAF)

A Firewall Controls Network Traffic according to defined Security Rules. A WAF helps protect Web Applications from certain types of Malicious HTTP Requests and Common Web Attacks. These systems require correct Configuration and regular Review.

10. Fraud Detection and Transaction Monitoring

Businesses should Monitor Unusual Transactions, repeated Failed Payments, unusual Locations and suspicious Purchase Patterns. Risk-based Verification and Alerts can help identify possible Fraud in time.

11. Regular Security Audit and Vulnerability Testing

A Security Audit identifies weaknesses and incorrect Configurations in a System. Security Controls can be checked through Authorized Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing. Identified Issues should be fixed according to their priority.

12. Backup and Recovery Plan

Important Data should be Backed Up regularly and securely. A Recovery Plan helps a Business restart its Services after an Incident or Ransomware Attack. Backups should be kept in a separate and secure location, and the Restore Process should be tested.

13. Employee and Customer Awareness

Employees should receive Training about Phishing, Social Engineering and Data Handling. Customers should be clearly informed that the Company will never ask them for an OTP or Password. They should be advised to be careful with Suspicious Links and Fake Messages.

Best Practices for Customers

  • Make Payments only on Official and HTTPS-enabled Websites.
  • Do not Share your OTP, UPI PIN, CVV or Password with anyone.
  • Avoid making Sensitive Transactions over Public Wi-Fi.
  • Verify the Merchant Name and Amount before making a Payment.
  • Keep Bank and Payment Alerts enabled and Report any suspicious Transaction immediately.

Q9. What is a Payment Gateway? Explain its Functions.

Payment Gateway

Payment Gateway A Payment Gateway is a Technology or Service that helps securely send an Online Payment made by a Customer on an E-Commerce Website or Application to the relevant Payment Processor, Bank or Payment Network, and return the Transaction Response to the Merchant. It acts as an important link between an Online Store and the Payment System.

A Payment Gateway does not always hold the money itself. The actual Authorization and Settlement process may be completed through parties such as the Payment Processor, Acquiring Bank, Issuing Bank and Payment Network. The technical arrangements may differ among Providers.

Example: When a Customer makes a Payment using a Debit Card on an Online Store, the Payment Gateway sends the Payment Details to the system that securely Processes them and provides the Store with the Success or Failure Status of the Transaction.

Main Parties Involved in Payment Gateway

Customer
The person who purchases an Online Product or Service.
Merchant
The Business or Online Store that sells a Product or Service.
Payment Gateway
Helps securely forward Payment Information and Transaction Responses.
Payment Processor
Processes the Transaction with the relevant Payment Network and Banks.
Acquiring Bank
The Bank or institution that accepts Card Payments on behalf of the Merchant.
Issuing Bank
The Bank that issues the Card to the Customer.
Payment Network
Provides the rules for Communication and Routing between Banks and other Participants in Card Transactions.

Working Process of Payment Gateway

  1. Product Selection:
    The Customer Selects a Product on the Website or App and proceeds to the Checkout Page.
  2. Payment Method Selection:
    The Customer chooses a Payment Method from the available options, such as Card, UPI or Net Banking.
  3. Secure Data Submission:
    Payment Details reach the Gateway through a Secure Payment Interface.
  4. Encryption and Security Checks:
    The Gateway and related Payment Systems use the applicable Security Controls and Risk Checks.
  5. Transaction Routing:
    The Payment Request is sent to the relevant Processor, Payment Network and Bank.
  6. Authorization:
    The Issuing Bank or relevant institution may Approve or Decline the Transaction based on the available Balance/Credit, Account Status and other Checks.
  7. Response:
    The Transaction Status reaches the Merchant Website through the Gateway.
  8. Order Confirmation:
    If the Payment is successful, the Merchant can Confirm the Order. If the Payment fails, an appropriate Message is shown to the Customer.
  9. Settlement:
    The amount reaches the Merchant's Account according to the Settlement Process of the relevant Financial Institutions.

Functions of Payment Gateway

Payment Data Collection
Helping obtain the required information about the Payment Method selected by the Customer.
Secure Transmission
Sending Payment Information to the relevant Systems through a Secure Channel.
Transaction Routing
Sending the Payment Request to the appropriate Processor or Payment Network.
Authorization Support
Helping obtain the Transaction Approval or Decline Response from the Bank.
Fraud Prevention
Helping identify suspicious Transactions through available Risk Checks and Fraud Detection Tools.
Multiple Payment Options
Providing the facility to accept Cards, UPI, Net Banking and other Methods according to the Provider's and Merchant's Integration.
Transaction Status
Providing the Merchant with Success, Failure or Pending Status.
Refund Support
Allowing the Merchant to initiate a Refund Request for supported Transactions.
Recurring Payments
Supporting Subscriptions or Recurring Payments where permitted by regulations and Customer Consent.
Reports and Records
Helping with Transaction History, Reports and Reconciliation.

Advantages of Payment Gateway

  • Convenience: It provides the Customer with an easy way to make Online Payments.
  • Faster Checkout: An organized Payment Process makes it easier to complete an Order.
  • Security Support: Encryption and other Security Controls help protect Payment Data.
  • Multiple Methods: Different Payment Options provide convenience to the Customer.
  • Business Reach: Accepting Online Payments can help a Business reach more Customers.
  • Transaction Records: Payment Status and Reports help with Accounting and Reconciliation.

Limitations

A Payment Gateway may involve Setup or Transaction Fees. Internet Failure, Gateway Downtime, Bank Declines and Technical Errors can cause Payments to fail. A Merchant should choose a Gateway after checking the Provider's Security, Compliance, Pricing and Customer Support.

Q10. Define Digital Marketing and Explain its Objectives.

Digital Marketing

Digital Marketing Digital Marketing is the Marketing Process in which the Internet and Digital Technologies are used to Promote Products, Services or Brands. It uses Digital Channels such as Search Engines, Websites, Social Media, E-Mail, Mobile Applications, Online Advertisements and Video Platforms. Its objective is to deliver the right Message to the right Audience and achieve the Business's Marketing Goals.

Example: A Computer Institute runs an Advertisement for its Courses on Facebook, Promotes its Website on Google Search and sends E-Mails to Interested Students. These are all Digital Marketing Activities.

Objectives of Digital Marketing

Brand Awareness
Making the Brand Name, Products and Services known to more people. Social Media, Display Ads and Video Content can help increase Brand Recognition.
Website Traffic
Bringing Relevant Visitors to a Website. SEO, Search Ads, Social Media Posts and E-Mail Links are used for this purpose.
Lead Generation
Obtaining information about Potential Customers and their Interests. Leads can be generated through Contact Forms, Landing Pages, Offers and Newsletter Sign-ups.
Increase Sales
Increasing the sales of Products and Services through Digital Channels. Proper Audience Targeting and an easy Checkout Process help achieve this.
Customer Engagement
Maintaining regular Interaction with Customers. Engagement is increased through Comments, Messages, Polls, Videos and E-Mail.
Customer Retention
Retaining existing Customers and encouraging them to make purchases again. Personalized Offers and Useful Updates can help achieve this.
Target Audience Reach
Reaching Potential Customers based on Age Group, Location, Interests and other available Targeting Options.
Cost Efficiency
Running Campaigns according to the Budget and adjusting expenses based on Performance. However, the cost of Digital Marketing depends on the Channel and Competition.
Measurable Results
Measuring Campaign Performance through Metrics such as Clicks, Impressions, Leads, Conversions and Sales.
Market Expansion
Providing Local Businesses with an opportunity to reach Customers in other cities or countries.
Customer Feedback
Understanding Customer needs and problems through Reviews, Surveys and Social Media Comments.
Competitive Advantage
Making better Marketing Decisions by understanding Market Trends and Customer Behaviour.

Major Channels of Digital Marketing

1. Search Engine Optimization (SEO)

SEO is used to Optimize a Website so that it gains better Organic Visibility in Search Engine Results. This involves working on Factors such as Keywords, Quality Content, Technical Structure and Backlinks.

2. Search Engine Marketing (SEM)

SEM reaches Users through Paid Advertisements on Search Engines. For example, displaying an Advertisement on Google Ads for a Relevant Search Query.

3. Social Media Marketing

Publishing Content, Interacting with the Audience and running Paid Promotions on Platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube is called Social Media Marketing.

4. Content Marketing

Useful Content such as Blogs, Articles, Guides, Infographics and Videos is created to attract and Educate the Audience.

5. E-Mail Marketing

Offers, Newsletters, Product Updates and Useful Information are sent to Customers or Subscribers through E-Mail. Consent and Privacy must be considered.

6. Mobile Marketing

Marketing is carried out through Mobile Devices using SMS, Mobile Apps, Push Notifications and Mobile-Friendly Websites.

7. Video Marketing

Messages are delivered to the Audience through Product Demonstrations, Tutorials, Customer Stories and Promotional Videos.

Advantages of Digital Marketing

  • The ability to reach a Large and Targeted Audience.
  • The ability to Measure Campaign Performance in Real Time or at regular intervals.
  • Direct Communication and Engagement with Customers.
  • Different Budget Options are available even for Small Businesses.
  • The ability to Update Content and Campaigns quickly.

Q11. Explain the Different Activities of Digital Marketing.

Digital Marketing Activities are all the planned tasks through which a Business uses Digital Channels to Promote its Products, Services or Brand. These Activities may include Audience Research, Content Creation, SEO, Online Advertising, Social Media Promotion, E-Mail Campaigns and Performance Analysis.

Digital Marketing Activities A Coaching Institute creates its Website, performs SEO on Google, uploads Demo Classes on YouTube and sends E-Mail Updates to Students. These are Digital Marketing Activities.

Example: In this Activity, a Business collects information about its Market, Competitors and Potential Customers. The Target Audience is identified by understanding Age, Location, Interests, Needs and Buying Behaviour. This helps make the Marketing Message more Relevant.

Major Activities of Digital Marketing

1. Market Research and Audience Analysis

A Business Website is an important foundation of Digital Marketing. The Website should contain clear Information, Product Details, Contact Options and easy Navigation. It should be Mobile-Friendly, Fast and Secure. Landing Pages are prepared according to the Campaign objective.

2. Website Development and Optimization

In SEO, a Website and its Content are Optimized for Search Engines. Keyword Research, Title Tags, Meta Descriptions, Heading Structure, Internal Links, Image Alt Text and Technical Improvements are part of this process. Its objective is to increase Organic Search Visibility and Relevant Traffic.

3. Search Engine Optimization (SEO)

This Activity involves running Paid Search Campaigns on Platforms such as Google Ads. The Advertiser selects Keywords, creates Ad Copy, decides the Budget and Bidding Strategy, and adds a Landing Page. The Campaign's Clicks and Conversions are Monitored.

4. Search Engine Marketing (SEM) and Paid Search Ads

Creating a Business Profile on Platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube, publishing Regular Posts, Sharing Videos, replying to Comments and running Paid Ads are part of Social Media Marketing. This can increase Brand Awareness and Customer Engagement.

5. Social Media Marketing

The Audience's problems are addressed by creating Useful and Relevant Content. Examples include Blog Posts, How-to Guides, Infographics, Case Studies and Educational Videos. Quality Content can help build Audience Trust and support SEO.

6. Content Marketing and Blogging

Newsletters, Product Updates, Offers and Follow-up Messages are sent through Permission-based E-Mail Lists. Attention should be paid to the Subject Line, Useful Content, Personalization and a Clear Call-to-Action. The Unsubscribe option and Privacy Rules must also be followed.

7. E-Mail Marketing

Mobile Marketing uses SMS, Mobile Applications, Push Notifications and Mobile-Friendly Campaigns. Messages should be Relevant and timely so that Users are not disturbed unnecessarily.

8. Mobile Marketing

Products or Services are explained through Product Demos, Tutorials, Explainer Videos, Customer Testimonials and Live Videos. Videos can be Published on YouTube, Social Media and Websites.

9. Video Marketing

Display Advertising shows Banner, Image or Rich Media Ads on Websites and Apps. It can be used for Brand Awareness, Product Promotion and Reaching Website Visitors again.

10. Display Advertising

Influencer Marketing Promotes a Product or Brand through a Relevant Creator. In Affiliate Marketing, a Partner may receive a Commission on an Eligible Sale made through their Referral Link or Code. Promotions should include clear Disclosure.

11. Influencer and Affiliate Marketing

Lead Generation collects Contact Details from Interested Users. Forms, Downloadable Guides, Demo Requests and Newsletter Sign-ups are used for this purpose. Conversion Optimization improves Forms, Checkout and Landing Pages so that more Visitors complete the desired Action.

12. Lead Generation and Conversion Optimization

Users, Traffic Sources, Engagement and Conversions are studied with the help of Google Analytics and other Analytics Tools. Campaign Reports show which Channel is performing well and where improvements are needed.

13. Analytics and Performance Monitoring

Set Business Goals and Measurable Objectives.

Steps for Planning Digital Marketing Activities

  1. Study the Target Audience and Customer Needs.
  2. Choose suitable Digital Channels.
  3. Prepare the Content, Budget and Campaign Schedule.
  4. Launch the Campaign and Monitor it regularly.
  5. Evaluate the Results and make improvements based on Analytics.
  6. It is possible to reach a Large Audience across different Digital Platforms.

5. Advantages of Digital Marketing Activities

  • Better Reach: Campaigns can be prepared for a Relevant Audience.
  • Targeting: Direct Interaction with Customers is possible.
  • Engagement: Results can be understood through Performance Metrics.
  • Measurement: Campaigns can be Updated and Optimized as required.
  • Flexibility: Google AdWords is the former name of Google's Online Advertising Platform, which is now called Google Ads.

Q12. How Would You Set Up a Google AdWords Campaign?

Google AdWords

Google AdWords Google AdWords is the former name Google Ads of Google's Online Advertising Platform, which is now

called Google Ads. With its help, Businesses can display Paid Advertisements on Google Search Results, YouTube, Websites and other Google Advertising Networks. The Advertiser can determine the Campaign Goal, Audience, Budget, Keywords and Ad Content.

Example: If a Computer Institute wants its Advertisement to appear to people Searching for "Computer Course in Kanpur", it can create a Search Campaign in Google Ads.

Steps to Set Up a Google Ads Campaign

Step 1: Create or Sign In to Google Ads Account

First, visit the Official Google Ads Website and Sign In with a Google Account. Enter the required Business information and Billing Details correctly. Carefully choose the available options during Account Setup.

Step 2: Define Campaign Objective

Decide the objective before creating the Campaign. For example, increase Website Traffic, obtain more Leads, increase Online Sales or improve Brand Awareness. Based on the Objective, Google Ads may show suitable Campaign Settings and Recommendations.

Step 3: Choose Campaign Type

Choose a Campaign Type according to your Goal. Major options may include Search, Display, Video, Shopping, App and Performance Max. Not all Types may be equally available for every Business or Account.

Search Campaign
To display Text Ads on Google Search.
Display Campaign
To display Visual Ads on Websites and Apps.
Video Campaign
To run Ads on YouTube and available Video Inventory.
Shopping Campaign
To Promote Products from an Eligible Product Catalogue.
App Campaign
To promote Mobile App Installs and App Engagement.
Performance Max
To run Ads across multiple Google Channels through a single Campaign.

Step 4: Set Campaign Name and Settings

Give the Campaign a clear Name so that it can be identified. Choose the Location, Language, Start and End Dates and other required Settings. Target only those Locations where the Business can actually provide its Service.

Step 5: Set Budget and Bidding Strategy

Set a Daily Budget to decide how much the Campaign should spend on average per day. Then choose a Bidding Strategy according to the Goal. For example, the Campaign can Focus on Clicks, Conversions or Conversion Value. Set the Budget and Bid according to the Business's capacity and Expected Results.

Step 6: Select Keywords

In a Search Campaign, choose Keywords that Target Customers may Search for. A Tool such as Keyword Planner can provide Search Ideas and estimated Data. Choose Relevant Keywords and add Negative Keywords so that the Ad does not appear for unnecessary Searches.

Step 7: Create Ad Groups

Organize Related Keywords and Ads into Ad Groups. Each Ad Group should have a clear topic. For example, a Computer Institute can place Keywords related to "Basic Computer Course" and "Programming Course" in separate Groups.

Step 8: Write Effective Ad Copy

Write an Ad that is relevant to the User's Search Intent. Mention the main Service or Benefit in the Headline. Include necessary information, features and a Call-to-Action such as "Apply Now" or "Learn More" in the Description. Avoid false or misleading Claims.

Step 9: Add Landing Page

The Page a User reaches after clicking an Ad is called a Landing Page. This Page should be related to the Ad's Message, Mobile-Friendly, Fast and easy to use. Providing a Contact Form, Course Details and a clear Call-to-Action is useful.

Step 10: Review and Publish

Recheck the Campaign Settings, Keywords, Budget, Ads, Landing Page and Billing Information. Ensure that Google Ads Policies are followed and then Submit the Campaign. After the Ad Review, the Campaign may start running if it is Eligible.

How to Monitor and Optimize the Campaign

Impressions
To understand how many times the Ad was displayed.
Clicks
How many Users clicked the Ad.
CTR
The percentage of Clicks compared with Impressions.
CPC
The average Cost of each Click.
Conversions
How many Users completed the desired Action, such as submitting a Form or making a Purchase.
Conversion Rate
The percentage of Clicks or other defined Interactions that resulted in a Conversion, according to the Measurement Setup.
Search Terms
Review actual Search Queries and add Irrelevant Keywords as Negative Keywords.
Landing Page Performance
Improve the Page Speed, Content and Form.

A Campaign should not be considered successful based only on Clicks. Depending on the Business Goal, the Quality of Leads, Sales, Cost per Conversion and Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) should also be evaluated.

Advantages of Google Ads

  • An opportunity to reach Users who perform Relevant Searches.
  • Targeting based on Location, Language and other available Options.
  • Control over Budget and Bidding.
  • Performance Reports and Conversion Tracking.
  • The ability to Pause, Edit and Optimize the Campaign as needed.
