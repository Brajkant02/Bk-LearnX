Unit 1 – E-Commerce

5 PYQ

Q1. Explain the Advantages and Disadvantages of E-Commerce.

E-Commerce

E-Commerce The full form is Electronic Commerce. It means buying and selling Goods and Services through the Internet and Electronic Networks. It includes Activities such as Online Shopping, Electronic Payments, Order Processing, Online Banking and Customer Support. Through E-Commerce, a Customer can purchase Products and Services from different Sellers at any time while sitting at home.

Example: When a Customer orders a Mobile Phone from Amazon or Flipkart, makes an Online Payment and receives it at home, this is an example of E-Commerce.

In E-Commerce, the Buyer, Seller, Online Platform, Internet, Payment Gateway and Delivery System play important roles. It does not limit a Business to a particular city or country; instead, it provides an opportunity to reach a wider Market.

Advantages of E-Commerce

  • Global Reach:
    Through E-Commerce, a Business can reach Customers in different cities and countries with its Products. This increases the size of its Market.
  • 24×7 Availability:
    Online Stores are not restricted to fixed opening hours like ordinary shops. Customers can view Products and place Orders at any time.
  • Convenience:
    Customers do not need to travel or stand in long queues to purchase goods. They can shop from home or the Office.
  • Lower Operating Cost:
    Many Online Businesses do not require an expensive shop, a large Showroom or a large number of Staff. This can reduce some Operating Expenses.
  • Product Variety: Products from many Brands and Sellers are available on a single Platform. Customers can compare Prices, Features and Reviews.
  • Easy Price Comparison:
    It is easy to compare prices from different Sellers on Online Platforms. This helps Customers choose a better option.
  • Personalization:
    Websites can display Relevant Products and Recommendations based on a Customer's Search and Purchase History.
  • Faster Transactions:
    Digital Payments and Automated Order Processing allow many Transactions to be completed quickly.
  • Better Business Information:
    Analytics Tools can provide Business Owners with information about Visitors, Popular Products and Customer Behaviour.
  • Scalability:
    With the right Technology and Logistics, an Online Business can expand into new Products and Markets.

Disadvantages of E-Commerce

  • Lack of Physical Inspection:
    A Customer cannot touch, wear or physically inspect a Product before purchasing it. Online Images do not always provide an experience identical to the actual Product.
  • Security and Privacy Risks:
    Problems such as Fake Websites, Phishing, Data Theft and Payment Fraud may occur. Therefore, Secure Payments and Data Protection are essential.
  • Dependence on Internet:
    Internet and Digital Devices are necessary for Online Shopping. A Network or Website problem can interrupt a Transaction.
  • Delivery Delay:
    The Product is not received immediately. Delivery may be delayed due to weather, distance, Transport or Logistics problems.
  • Return and Refund Problems:
    Sometimes, the process of Return, Replacement or Refund can be difficult or time-consuming.
  • Technical Issues:
    Server Failure, Website Errors and Payment Gateway malfunctions can affect the Customer Experience.
  • High Competition:
    In an Online Market, Customers have many options. It can be difficult for a new Business to establish its identity.
  • Limited Personal Interaction:
    Customers do not always receive Face-to-Face Advice and Personal Service like they do in a physical shop.
  • Shipping Cost:
    Delivery Charges may be high for some Orders, especially in remote areas.

Examples of E-Commerce

Amazon and Flipkart are examples of Product Marketplaces. Myntra provides Online Fashion Shopping. Online Train Tickets can be booked through IRCTC. The Websites and Apps of many Banks provide Internet Banking and Digital Services.

Q2. Explain the C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer) Business Model of E-Commerce.

C2C

C2C The full form is Consumer-to-Consumer. It is an E-Commerce Business Model in which one Consumer directly sells Goods or Services to another Consumer. In this Model, an Online Platform acts as a medium for establishing contact between the Buyer and Seller and providing facilities such as Listing, Communication and sometimes Payment or Delivery.

In the C2C Model, the Platform generally connects the Buyer and Seller. Many Platforms provide only Listing and Messaging facilities, while some also offer Services such as Payment Protection, Delivery Support or Dispute Resolution. The Platform's role depends on its rules and facilities.

Example: A person creates a Listing on OLX to sell an old Bicycle, and another person buys that Bicycle from them. This is an example of C2C E-Commerce.

Main Parties in C2C Model

Seller
A Consumer who creates a Listing to sell a Product or Service.
Buyer
A Consumer who purchases a Product or Service from another Consumer.
Online Platform
A Website or Application that connects the Buyer and Seller.
Payment Service
Where available, it helps receive and send Payments securely.
Delivery Partner
In some Transactions, a Courier or Logistics Company delivers the Product to the Buyer.

Working Process of C2C

  1. Registration:
    The Seller or Buyer creates an Account on the Online Platform and enters the required information.
  2. Product Listing:
    The Seller prepares a Listing with the Product's Photos, Description, Condition and Expected Price.
  3. Product Search:
    The Buyer searches for a Product on the Platform according to their needs and views the available Listings.
  4. Communication:
    The Buyer and Seller discuss Product information and Price through Chat, Calls or the Platform's Messaging System.
  5. Price Negotiation:
    On many C2C Platforms, the Buyer and Seller can decide the Price by mutual agreement.
  6. Payment:
    Payment can be made through Cash, Bank Transfer, UPI or a Payment Method provided by the Platform. The Payment Method affects the Safety of the Transaction.
  7. Delivery or Handover:
    The Product can be handed over in person or sent through a Courier.
  8. Feedback:
    On some Platforms, the Buyer and Seller can give each other a Rating or Review.

Features of C2C Model

  • Direct Consumer Interaction:
    The Buyer and Seller can communicate directly.
  • Online Listing:
    Consumers prepare the Listings and Descriptions of Products themselves.
  • Flexible Pricing:
    Price Negotiation is available in many cases.
  • Wide Reach:
    Users across a wide area can view a Listing through the Internet.
  • Used and New Products:
    Both Second-hand and new Products can be sold in this Model.
  • Platform Support:
    The Platform can provide Search, Messaging, Ratings and other facilities.

Advantages of C2C

  • Low Cost: The Seller does not need to create a separate Online Store.
  • Extra Income: Consumers can earn money by selling old or unused goods.
  • Affordable Products: Buyers can often find Second-hand Products at lower prices.
  • Convenience: Listing and Searching can be done from home.
  • Reuse of Products: Reusing goods can help reduce Waste.

Disadvantages and Risks of C2C

  • Fraud Risk: Fake Listings and fraudulent Buyers or Sellers can commit fraud.
  • Quality Issues: The actual Condition of a Product may differ from its Description.
  • Limited Warranty: Goods purchased from an Individual Seller may not have a Warranty or After-Sales Service.
  • Payment Disputes: Disputes may arise regarding Payment and Delivery.
  • Trust Problem: The Buyer and Seller do not know each other personally, so building trust can be challenging.

Examples of C2C Platforms

Consumers can create Listings for Used Products on OLX and Facebook Marketplace. Individual Sellers can also sell their Products on eBay. The facilities and Transaction Rules of these Platforms may differ.

Safety Measures

The Buyer should check the Product's Condition, review the Seller's Profile and Reviews, and avoid suspicious Advance Payments. The Seller should also avoid handing over the Product without checking Payment Confirmation. Personal information and OTPs should not be shared with anyone.

Q3. Explain Supply Chain Management (SCM).

Supply Chain Management

Supply Chain Management (SCM) It is the Process through which Activities related to the Purchase of Raw Materials, Production, Storage, Transportation and Delivery of a Product or Service to the Final Customer are Planned, Managed and Controlled. Its objective is to deliver the right Product to the right Customer, at the right time and place, and at an appropriate Cost.

SCM makes this entire chain effective. It manages the Flow of Information and Products among the Manufacturer, Supplier, Distributor, Retailer, Logistics Company and Customer. Modern Businesses also use Software, Tracking Systems and Data Analytics for SCM.

Example: To manufacture a Mobile Phone, the Screen, Battery and Processor are obtained from different Suppliers. The Phone is assembled in a Factory, stored in a Warehouse and delivered to the Customer through a Distributor and Retailer. The Management of all these Activities is called SCM.

Main Components of SCM

Planning
Estimating Customer Demand, deciding the Production quantity and planning Resources.
Sourcing
Selecting suitable Suppliers for Raw Materials and other required Items and purchasing from them.
Manufacturing
Converting Raw Materials into a finished Product. This includes Production, Quality Checking and Packaging.
Inventory Management
Maintaining an appropriate Stock of Raw Materials and Finished Goods so that there is neither a shortage nor unnecessary excess Stock.
Warehousing
Storing Products safely and Dispatching them as required.
Transportation
Transporting Materials and Products between the Supplier, Factory, Warehouse and Customer.
Order Fulfilment
Receiving the Customer's Order, Processing it, Packing it and Delivering it on time.
Returns Management
The process of taking back Damaged, Defective or Returned Products and resolving them appropriately.

Working Process of SCM

  1. Demand Forecasting:
    The Business estimates how many Products will be required based on previous Sales Data and Market Trends.
  2. Supplier Selection:
    Suppliers are selected based on Quality, Price and Reliability.
  3. Procurement:
    Orders are placed for the required Raw Materials and Components.
  4. Production:
    The Product is prepared using the received Materials and a Quality Check is performed.
  5. Storage:
    Finished Products are stored systematically in a Warehouse.
  6. Distribution:
    Products are sent to Distributors, Retailers or Fulfilment Centres.
  7. Delivery:
    The Product is delivered to the Final Customer.
  8. Feedback and Returns:
    The Supply Chain is improved using Customer Feedback and information about Returns.

Types of Supply Chain Flow

Material Flow
The movement of Raw Materials and Finished Products from one place to another.
Information Flow
The exchange of information related to Orders, Demand, Stock and Delivery.
Financial Flow
The flow of Payments, Invoices, Credit and other Financial Transactions.

Importance and Advantages of SCM

  • Cost Reduction: Better Planning and Transportation can reduce unnecessary expenses.
  • Timely Delivery: With proper Coordination, Products reach Customers on time.
  • Inventory Control: Both Stock Shortages and Excess Stock problems are reduced.
  • Customer Satisfaction: The right Product and timely Delivery improve the Customer Experience.
  • Better Coordination: Communication among the Supplier, Manufacturer and Retailer improves.
  • Improved Productivity: An organized Process leads to better use of Resources.
  • Business Competitiveness: Lower Cost and better Service make a Business competitive in the Market.

Challenges of SCM

SCM may face challenges such as inaccurate Demand Forecasting, Supplier delays, increased Transport Costs, natural disasters, Stock shortages, Product Damage and problems with Information Sharing. Customs, Regulations and Currency Changes also affect International Supply Chains. Multiple Suppliers, Accurate Forecasting, Real-Time Tracking and Risk Planning are useful for reducing these challenges.

Q4. Explain the Different Types of E-Commerce Business Models.

E-Commerce Business Model

E-Commerce Business Model It is the method through which a Business uses the Internet to exchange Products or Services and generate Revenue. It explains who the Buyer and Seller are in an Online Transaction and how Business takes place between them.

Example: On Amazon, a Company sells a Product directly to a Customer, while on OLX, one Consumer can sell an old item to another Consumer. Both are E-Commerce, but their Business Models are different.

Major Types of E-Commerce Business Models

1. B2B (Business-to-Business)

In the B2B Model, one Business sells Products or Services to another Business Organization. It often includes Bulk Orders, Wholesale Supply and Long-Term Contracts.

Example: A Manufacturer Supplies goods in large quantities to a Retail Store. Platforms such as Alibaba help Businesses find Suppliers.

Features: Bulk Transactions, Negotiated Pricing, Business Accounts and Long-Term Relationships.

2. B2C (Business-to-Consumer)

In the B2C Model, a Business sells a Product or Service directly to the Final Consumer. It is the most familiar Model of Online Shopping. The Customer selects a Product on a Website or App, then places an Order and makes Payment.

Example: A Customer purchasing Shoes or a Mobile Phone from Amazon, Flipkart or a Brand's Official Website.

Features: Product Catalogue, Online Payment, Customer Reviews, Offers and Home Delivery.

3. C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer)

In the C2C Model, one Consumer sells a Product or Service to another Consumer. The Online Platform acts as a medium for connecting them.

Example: A person sells an old Bicycle or Furniture to another person on OLX.

Features: Individual Sellers, Online Listings, Direct Communication and, in many cases, Price Negotiation.

4. C2B (Consumer-to-Business)

In the C2B Model, an Individual Consumer or person provides a Service, Skill or Product to a Business. The individual receives Payment from the Business based on their ability.

Example: A Freelance Graphic Designer designs a Logo for a Company and receives Payment in return. Similarly, a Photographer can License their Images to a Business.

Features: Freelancing, Content Creation, Professional Services and Flexible Pricing.

5. B2G (Business-to-Government)

In the B2G Model, a Business Organization provides Products and Services to a Government Department or Public Sector Organization. It may include processes such as Tendering, Contracts and Government Procurement.

Example: An IT Company Supplies Software or Computer Equipment to a Government Department.

6. G2C (Government-to-Citizen)

In the G2C Model, the Government provides Services to Citizens through Online channels. Its objective is to make Government Services more convenient and accessible.

Example: Online Certificate Applications, Electricity Bill Payments, Applications on Government Portals and Online Tax Services.

7. G2B (Government-to-Business)

In the G2B Model, the Government provides Online Services and Information to Businesses. These may include Business Registration, License Applications, Tax Filing and Compliance Services.

Advantages of E-Commerce Models

  • Wider Market: Businesses and Consumers can connect with people in distant locations through the Internet.
  • Convenience: Transactions can be carried out from home or the Office.
  • Faster Communication: Orders and Information are exchanged quickly on Online Platforms.
  • Cost Efficiency: Making many Processes Digital can save time and some Operating Costs.
  • More Opportunities: Freelancers, Small Businesses and Individual Sellers can reach new Customers.

Limitations

These Models may face challenges such as Cyber Fraud, Data Privacy, Payment Security, Technical Failure and Delivery. Some Transactions also involve legal rules, Tax and Verification requirements. Therefore, Secure Platforms and clear Business Policies are essential.

Q5. What is E-Governance? Explain its Advantages.

E-Governance

E-Governance It means Electronic Governance. It is the Process in which the Government uses Information and Communication Technology (ICT), the Internet and Digital Platforms to deliver Government Services, Information and Administration to Citizens, Businesses and other Government Departments. Its main objective is to make Government operations more transparent, faster, more convenient and more effective.

E-Governance uses Websites, Mobile Applications, Digital Databases, Online Payment Systems and Communication Networks. This allows Citizens to access Government information and Services easily. However, effective use requires Internet Access, Digital Literacy, Data Security and strong Technical Infrastructure.

Example: When a Citizen applies for an Online Birth Certificate through a Government Portal or pays an Electricity Bill from home, this is an example of E-Governance.

Types of E-Governance

G2C (Government-to-Citizen)
The Government provides Online Services directly to Citizens. Examples: Birth Certificates, Online Pension Services, Applications for Government Schemes and Utility Bill Payments.
G2B (Government-to-Business)
The Government provides Digital Services to Businesses. Examples: Business Registration, License Applications, Online Tax Filing and Tender Information.
G2G (Government-to-Government)
Electronic Exchange of Information and Data between different Government Departments or Agencies. Example: Secure exchange of Records and Reports between two Departments.
G2E (Government-to-Employee)
The Government provides Online facilities to its Employees. Examples: Salary Slips, Leave Applications, Service Records and Internal Communication Portals.

Major Features of E-Governance

  • Digital Services: Government Services are available through Websites and Mobile Apps.
  • Online Information: Citizens can obtain information about Government Schemes, Rules and Application Procedures.
  • Electronic Records: Documents and Records can be Stored and Managed in Digital form.
  • Online Communication: Communication related to complaints, suggestions and Applications becomes easier between Citizens and Government Departments.
  • Digital Payments: Where the facility is available, Fees and Bills can be paid Online.

Advantages of E-Governance

  • Transparency: Viewing Application Status, Rules and available information on Online Portals can increase transparency in Government processes.
  • Time Saving: The need to visit an Office for many Services is reduced. This saves Citizens' time.
  • Cost Reduction: Paperwork, Printing and some Administrative Expenses can be reduced. Citizens' Travel Expenses may also decrease.
  • Convenience: Available Online Services can be used from home or another location. Some Services are available 24×7.
  • Better Efficiency: Digital Records and Automated Processes allow Applications to be Processed systematically.
  • Accountability: Application Tracking and Digital Records can help establish accountability.
  • Reduced Paperwork: The use of Electronic Forms and Digital Documents reduces dependence on paper.
  • Improved Communication: Citizens can receive Notifications, Updates and Government Announcements through Digital channels.
  • Wider Access: People in remote areas can also access many Government Services when Internet access is available.
  • Better Planning: Organized Digital Data can help the Government plan Schemes and Public Services.

Examples of E-Governance in India

DigiLocker
A facility to securely Access and Share Digital Documents.
UMANG
A Platform that provides various Government Services through a single Mobile Application.
GST Portal
An Online Portal for GST Registration, Return Filing and related Tax Services.
Passport Seva
Digital Services for Passport Applications and related processes.
Online Utility Payments
Bills for many Government and Public Utility Services can be paid Online.

Challenges of E-Governance

E-Governance faces challenges such as the Digital Divide, low Digital Literacy, weak Internet Connectivity, Cyber Attacks, Data Privacy and Technical Failures. Not all Citizens have access to a Smartphone, Computer or Internet. Therefore, Help Centres and other alternatives are also necessary alongside Online Services. The Government should focus on Strong Cybersecurity, Data Protection, User-Friendly Design and regular System Updates.
