Q23. What is Google Analytics? Explain Its Uses.

Google Analytics

Google Analytics is a Web Analytics Tool used to Measure and Analyze Users and their Behaviour on a Website or App. Its current major version is Google Analytics 4 (GA4). It Collects Data based on Events, such as Page Views, Button Clicks, Form Submissions and Purchases.

If Students view Course Pages, fill out a Registration Form and Click a Contact Button on an Educational Website, Google Analytics can help Measure these Activities.

How Google Analytics Works

A Google Tag or Measurement Code is Configured on the Website or App. When a User performs an Activity on the Website, the related Events can be sent to Analytics. GA4 Processes these Events and displays them in Reports. For accurate Data, Tag Setup, Consent Requirements and Event Configuration must be correct.

Major Uses of Google Analytics

  1. Traffic Analysis:
    Information about Users and Sessions on the Website.
  2. Traffic Sources:
    Identifying Visitors coming from Organic Search, Direct, Referral, Social and Paid Campaigns.
  3. Audience Analysis:
    Understanding Users' Location, Device and other Characteristics according to available and consent-based Data.
  4. Page Performance:
    Which Pages are viewed more often and which Content is useful.
  5. Event Tracking:
    Measuring Activities such as Button Clicks, Video Interactions, File Downloads and Form Submissions.
  6. Conversion Tracking:
    Tracking important Actions such as Purchases, Registrations or Enquiries.
  7. Campaign Analysis:
    Comparing Traffic and Results generated by Marketing Campaigns.

Important Reports in GA4

Realtime Report:
An Overview of Activity currently taking place on the Website or App.
Acquisition Reports:
Information about the Channel or Source from which Users arrived.
Engagement Reports:
Details of Events, Pages and User Interactions.
Monetization Reports:
E-Commerce Revenue and Purchase-related Data, if Configured correctly.
Retention Reports:
Information related to Users returning to the Website.

Advantages

Google Analytics helps understand Website Performance, evaluate the Marketing Budget, improve User Experience and make Data-based Business Decisions. It provides various Reports and Custom Explorations.

Limitations and Precautions

Analytics Data depends on Tag Configuration, Consent, Browser Restrictions and Data Settings. Incorrect Setup can make Reports incomplete or inaccurate. Personal Information should not be Collected without an appropriate basis and permission. Consider limitations such as Sampled or Thresholded Data when interpreting Reports.

Q24. What Are the Main Channels Google Analytics Uses to Track Traffic Sources?

In Google Analytics, Traffic Source means the medium or place from which a User reached a Website or App. In GA4, Traffic can be understood using Dimensions such as Source, Medium and Default Channel Group. A Channel groups multiple Sources and Mediums into a broader Category.

Visitors reach a Website through different routes. Some come from Google Search, some from a Social Media Link, some from an Advertisement and others by typing the Website Address directly. By analyzing Traffic Channels, a Marketer can understand which Marketing Medium brings more Visitors or Conversions.

Main Traffic Channels

  1. Direct:
    Traffic for which Analytics does not have a clear Referrer or Campaign Source. This can include opening a Website from the Address Bar or clicking certain Links whose Source could not be identified.
  2. Organic Search:
    Traffic coming from Unpaid Results on a Search Engine.
  3. Paid Search:
    Traffic coming from Paid Search Advertisements run on a Search Engine.
  4. Organic Social:
    Visits coming from Links Shared on Social Media without Paid Promotion.
  5. Paid Social:
    Traffic coming from Paid Advertisements on Social Media.
  6. Referral:
    Visitors coming by clicking a Link on another Website.
  7. Email:
    Traffic coming from Links in an Email Campaign, if Tracking is configured correctly.
  8. Display:
    Traffic coming from Display Advertising Campaigns.
  9. Affiliates:
    Visits coming through Affiliate Links.
  10. Video:
    Traffic coming from Video Campaigns or Video-related Sources, according to Channel Rules.

Source and Medium

Source tells where the Traffic came from, such as Google, Facebook or the name of a Website. Medium tells what type of Traffic it was, such as Organic, CPC, Referral or Email. For example, Source = Google and Medium = Organic. Correctly understanding Source/Medium enables clearer Analysis of Campaign Performance.

How to View Channels in GA4

Open the Google Analytics Account and go to Acquisition Reports in the Reports Section. In the Traffic Acquisition Report, Metrics such as Session Default Channel Group, Session Source/Medium, Sessions, Engaged Sessions and Conversions can be viewed. Change the Date Range and Comparisons as needed.

Importance of Channel Analysis

Channel Analysis shows which Mediums are bringing more Traffic and Conversions. This makes it easier to use the Marketing Budget effectively, identify weak Campaigns and focus on better Channels. For example, if Organic Search brings more Visitors but Paid Social brings better Leads, the roles of both Channels can be understood separately.

Common Tracking Issues

Incorrectly written UTM Parameters, missing Tracking Information on Campaign Links, Parameters being removed during Redirects and incorrect Tag Configuration can cause Traffic to appear in the wrong Channel. Maintain a consistent Naming Convention and understand GA4 Channel Grouping Rules.

Q25. How Can You Measure the Success of a Marketing Campaign Using Google Analytics?

Marketing Campaign Measurement

Marketing Campaign Measurement is the Process of evaluating Campaign Results against defined Goals and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). Google Analytics can be used to Analyze Campaign Traffic, Engagement, Conversions and Revenue.

To call a Campaign successful, checking only Clicks or Impressions is not enough. The Campaign objective should be decided first. If the objective is Product Sales, Purchases and Revenue will be important. If the objective is to obtain Enquiries, Form Submissions and Qualified Leads will be more important.

Set Campaign Objectives and KPIs

First, set a clear Goal—Brand Awareness, Website Traffic, Lead Generation, App Activity or Sales. Choose KPIs accordingly. Users and Sessions may be useful for Awareness; Form Submit Events and Leads for a Lead Campaign; and Purchases, Revenue and Purchase Conversion Rate for a Sales Campaign.

Use UTM Parameters

Add UTM Parameters to Campaign Links so Analytics can identify which Campaign a Visitor came from. Common Parameters are utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign. For example, a Website Link in a Newsletter may use Source=email, Medium=newsletter and Campaign=diwali_offer. Maintain Consistent Naming for Parameters.

Analyze Acquisition Data

View Session Source/Medium and Session Default Channel Group in the GA4 Traffic Acquisition Report. This shows the Source and Channel from which Campaign Traffic arrived. Compare Campaigns using the same Date Range and Attribution Context.

Measure Engagement

View Engaged Sessions, Average Engagement Time, Events and Landing Page Performance in Engagement Reports. This helps determine whether Visitors from the Campaign are having useful Interactions on the Website. Do not consider a high number of Sessions alone as success; also check Traffic Relevance.

Track Conversions

Configure important User Actions as Events, such as Form Submit, Sign-up, Purchase or Contact Request. Events considered important for Business Success can be Marked as Key Events. Then review Key Events and Conversion Performance by Campaign in Reports.

Measure Revenue and ROI

In an E-Commerce Business, correctly Configure Purchase Revenue and Transaction Data. ROI can be calculated by connecting Campaign Cost with Analytics or other Advertising Reports. General Formula: ROI = (Profit from the Campaign − Campaign Cost) ÷ Campaign Cost × 100. Distinguish Revenue from Profit; actual Cost and Margin Data are necessary for better ROI Analysis.

Compare and Optimize

Set a Baseline and Target before starting the Campaign. Compare Results according to the Date Range. Identify weak Landing Pages, Low-converting Sources and high-Cost Campaigns. Improve the Ad Creative, Audience, Landing Page and Call-to-Action. Analyze the Data again after making changes.

Q26. What Are Conversions? How Can You Track Conversions Through Google Analytics?

Conversion

Conversion is an important Action that a Business wants a User to complete on its Website or App. This Action is related to a Business Goal. For example, an Online Purchase, Registration, Enquiry Form Submission, Subscription or Appointment Booking for a Service can be a Conversion.

Not every Visitor who comes to a Website becomes a Customer. Therefore, a Business needs to know how many Visitors completed the desired Action. Conversion Tracking helps understand Marketing Campaign Effectiveness, Website Usability and the Customer Journey.

Examples of Conversions

E-Commerce: Making a Product Purchase or completing Checkout.
Education Website: Submitting an Admission Enquiry Form or Course Registration.
Service Business: Booking an Appointment or sending a Contact Request.
Content Website: Subscribing to a Newsletter or Downloading an important Resource.
Every Business should define a Conversion according to its main objective.

How to Track Conversions in GA4

First, define the Conversion Goal. Then Configure a GA4 Event for that Action. Some Events may be Collected Automatically through Enhanced Measurement or Recommended Events, while others may require Setup through Google Tag Manager, Google Tag or Website Code. Keep the Event Name and Parameters clear.

Mark an Event as a Key Event

Once the relevant Event is available in the Events Section in GA4, it can be Marked as a Key Event. This identifies the Action as important for the Business and allows it to be Analyzed in Reports. The names of options in the Interface may change over time.

Verify Conversion Tracking

After Setup, use Tools such as the Realtime Report and DebugView to check whether the Event Fires at the correct time. Perform a Test Form Submission or Test Purchase. Check for Duplicate Events, incorrect Triggers and Missing Parameters. Test without affecting real Customer Data.

Analyze Conversion Reports

View Results in Reports according to Key Events, Traffic Source, Medium, Campaign and Landing Page. Interpret the Conversion Rate according to the selected basis. Since the definition of Conversion may differ across Business Models, keep the Report Context clear.

Benefits of Conversion Tracking

This shows which Campaigns are bringing actual Actions, which Pages are performing better and where there may be a problem in the Customer Journey. This can improve Budget Allocation, Landing Page Optimization and Marketing Decisions.

Common Mistakes

Common mistakes include an incorrect Event Name, Duplicate Tracking, treating only a Button Click as a Conversion instead of a Form Submission, including Test Events in real Data and ignoring Consent Requirements. A Conversion should be defined according to the Business Outcome.

Q27. What is Goal Conversion? Explain Its Importance.

Goal Conversion

Goal Conversion is the situation in which a Website or App User completes an important Goal defined by the Business. Examples include making a Purchase, completing Registration, submitting an Enquiry Form or Subscribing to a Newsletter. In GA4, such important Actions are Tracked as Events and Marked as Key Events.

Every Website has some objective. The objective of an E-Commerce Website may be Sales, while the objective of an Educational Website may be Admission Enquiries or Course Registrations. Goal Conversion enables a Business to measure how many Website Visitors are completing the desired Action.

Types of Goal Conversions

Destination/Completion Goal: Reaching an important Confirmation Page, such as a Thank You Page. This can be Tracked through a suitable Event in GA4.
Lead Generation Goal: Submitting an Enquiry Form or sending a Call Request.
Sales Goal: Purchasing a Product or completing a Payment.
Engagement Goal: Newsletter Sign-up, Account Creation or another important Interaction.
These are examples of Business Goals; they need to be Configured as Events in GA4.

Importance of Goal Conversion

Goal Conversion helps a Business measure Website Effectiveness. It shows whether Visitors are only viewing Pages or also taking actual Business Actions. It helps compare Marketing Campaigns, Analyze Lead Quality and identify problems in the Sales Funnel.

Steps to Configure in GA4

  1. First, define the Business Objective.
  2. Then select or Configure an Event that Represents that Action.
  3. The Event can be sent through Google Tag Manager or Google Tag.
  4. Check the Event in GA4 and Mark it as a Key Event.
  5. Perform Testing using Realtime and DebugView.
  6. Then view Key Events in Reports according to Traffic Source and Campaign.

Goal Conversion Rate

Conversion Rate tells what percentage of Users or Sessions completed a Conversion on the selected basis. For example, if a Form is Submitted in 50 out of 1,000 Sessions, the Session-based Conversion Rate is 5%. Clearly specify whether the Denominator uses Users or Sessions when calculating the Rate.

Example

An Online Course Website has the Goal of getting Visitors to Submit a Course Enquiry Form. The Website Owner Configures an Event such as generate_lead for Form Submission. After Testing, it is Marked as a Key Event. The Owner can now see which Channel—Organic Search, Social Media or Paid Ads—is bringing more Enquiries.

Benefits and Precautions

Goal Conversion helps measure Business Results, improve the Campaign Budget and improve Website Experience. However, do not consider only a Button Click a Successful Lead if the Form was not actually Submitted. Avoid Duplicate Events and incorrect Triggers. Follow User Consent and Privacy Rules.

Q28. How Would You Measure a Website's ROI?

Website ROI

ROI stands for Return on Investment. Website ROI measures how much Financial Return or Profit a Business receives compared with the expenses made on the Website. These expenses may include Website Development, Hosting, Content, Maintenance, SEO and Online Marketing.

A Website can be a medium for Sales, Leads, Bookings or Customer Support for a Business. Spending on a Website is useful only when it provides sufficient Value to the Business. ROI Measurement shows whether the Website Investment is Profitable or needs improvement.

Identify Website Goals

First, define the main objective of the Website. Goals may include Purchases and Revenue for an E-Commerce Website, Qualified Leads for a Service Website, Admissions or Enquiries for an Educational Website and Subscriptions for a Content Website.

Calculate Total Investment

Add all Relevant Costs incurred on the Website. These may include Domain and Hosting, Design and Development, Content Creation, Technical Maintenance, SEO Services, Paid Advertising and necessary Software Tools. Choose a fixed period, such as Monthly or Annual, so that Cost and Return are compared over the same period.

Measure Website Returns

Measure Revenue, Online Sales, Booked Services and the actual Business Value generated from Website-generated Leads. If it is a Lead Generation Website, do not assign the same Revenue to every Lead. Estimate Value using the Lead-to-Customer Conversion Rate and Average Customer Value.

ROI Formula

The general ROI Formula is:
ROI (%) = [(Return − Investment Cost) ÷ Investment Cost] × 100
Example: If the Net Return related to a Website during a period is ₹1,50,000 and the total Investment is ₹1,00,000, then ROI = (₹1,50,000 − ₹1,00,000) ÷ ₹1,00,000 × 100 = 50%. The definition of Return must be clear; Revenue and Profit should not be treated as the same.

Tools and Data Sources

Use Google Analytics 4 to view Website Traffic, Sources, Events and Key Events. Use Google Search Console to understand Organic Search Performance. Obtain Ad Spend and Campaign Results from Advertising Platforms. Confirm Lead Quality, Closed Sales and actual Revenue through CRM or Sales Records.

Important Metrics

  1. Conversion Rate:
    How many Visitors or Sessions completed the Desired Action.
  2. Cost per Lead:
    The average Cost of obtaining one Lead.
  3. Customer Acquisition Cost: The Cost of acquiring a new Customer.
  4. Revenue:
    The value of Sales associated with the Website.
  5. Average Order Value:
    The average Sales Value per Order.
  6. Lead-to-Customer Rate:
    The ratio of Leads that become Customers.

Challenges and Precautions

Measuring the actual Contribution of a Website can be difficult because of Offline Sales and Multi-Channel Customer Journeys. Attribution Models, Tracking Gaps and Duplicate Conversions can affect Results. Traffic or Page Views alone should not be considered Financial Return.

Q29. How Does Google Search Console Help Improve Website Performance?

Google Search Console (GSC)

Google Search Console (GSC) is a Free Service from Google that helps Website Owners Monitor and Maintain their Website's Presence in Google Search. It provides information related to Search Performance, Indexing Status, Sitemaps and some Technical Issues.

After Publishing a Website, it is important to ensure that the Search Engine can discover and understand its Pages. Google Search Console tells Website Owners how Pages are performing in Search Results and which Pages may have Indexing or Technical Problems.

Main Features of Google Search Console

Performance Report:
Displays Search Queries, Clicks, Impressions, CTR and Average Position.
URL Inspection:
Helps check the Indexing Status of a URL and available Crawl Information.
Page Indexing Report:
Provides information about the reasons Pages are Indexed or Not Indexed.
Sitemaps:
Submitting an XML Sitemap helps Google discover Website URLs.
Core Web Vitals:
Displays Page Experience-related Indicators based on available Field Data.
Links Report:
Provides information about the Website's Internal and External Links.
Manual Actions and Security Issues:
Can report certain types of Search Policy or Security Problems.

How It Helps Improve Performance

The Performance Report shows which Queries are bringing Impressions and Clicks to the Website. Titles and Snippets of Relevant Pages with a low CTR can be improved. Indexing Reports identify Pages that Google is unable to Index. Improving the Sitemap and Internal Linking can help Google discover Important Pages. The Core Web Vitals Report can identify User Experience-related problems.

Steps to Use Search Console

  1. Add the Website Property in Google Search Console.
  2. Choose a Domain or URL Prefix Property and Verify Ownership.
  3. Submit the XML Sitemap. Analyze Queries and Pages in the Performance Report.
  4. Check Important Pages using URL Inspection.
  5. Review Issues in the Indexing and Experience Reports.
  6. After making improvements, Request Re-indexing through URL Inspection if needed; this does not Guarantee Indexing.

Practical Example

Suppose an Institute's Website is receiving Impressions for the Query “PGDCA Course” but has few Clicks. The Owner checks the Query and Page in the Performance Report. The Owner makes the Page Title and Description clearer, adds Course Details to the Content and then compares Performance after some time.

Advantages

GSC helps understand Search Visibility, identify Indexing Problems, Submit Sitemaps, Analyze Search Queries and improve Website Health. It provides First-party Information related to Google Search.

Limitations

Search Console does not show Data from all Search Engines. Some Data in Reports may be Anonymized or Limited. Average Position does not indicate a fixed Rank for every User. Results may take time even after an Issue is Fixed.

Q30. What is Google Tag Manager? Explain Its Uses.

Google Tag Manager

Google Tag Manager (GTM) is a Tag Management System that helps organize and Deploy Tracking Tags and related Code on a Website or App. It can reduce the need to repeatedly change the Website Source Code for every small Tracking Change.

In Digital Marketing, it is necessary to Track User Actions on a Website. For example, Google Analytics Events, Advertising Conversion Tags and Remarketing Tags can be installed. Google Tag Manager helps Manage these Tags through a Central Interface.

Main Components of GTM

Tags:
Codes or Tracking Configurations that send Data, such as a GA4 Event Tag or Advertising Tag.
Triggers:
Conditions that cause a Tag to Fire when they are met, such as a Page View, Button Click or Form Submission.
Variables:
Values that provide additional information to Tags and Triggers, such as Page URL, Click Text or Event Name.
Data Layer:
A method of providing Structured Data between a Website and Tag Manager.

Uses of Google Tag Manager

GTM can be used to Setup Google Analytics Tracking. Events such as Button Clicks, Form Submissions and Downloads can be Tracked. Advertising Conversion Tags and Remarketing Tags can be Managed. Marketing Teams can Test Tracking Changes using Versioning and Preview Mode. This can reduce the workload of the Development Team for small Tracking Updates.

How Google Tag Manager Works

  1. First, a GTM Account and Container are created.
  2. The Container Code is Installed on the Website.
  3. Then the required Tags, Triggers and Variables are Configured.
  4. Use Preview Mode to check whether the Tag Fires on the correct Page and Action.
  5. Publish the Workspace once everything is correct.
  6. Changes can be reviewed later through Version History.

Example: Track a Button Click

Suppose there is a “Contact Us” Button on a Website. Configure a Click Trigger in GTM that identifies that Button. Then create a GA4 Event Tag and send an Event Name such as contact_click. Test in Preview Mode to ensure that the Event Fires once when the correct Button is clicked. Then Publish and Verify the Event in GA4.

Advantages

The main benefits include Centralized Tag Management, Faster Tracking Updates, Preview and Debugging, Version History and the ability to Manage Tags for various Marketing Tools. Correct Configuration can make Tracking more organized.

Precautions and Limitations

An incorrect Trigger can send Duplicate or Unwanted Events. GTM does not automatically fix all Website Code Problems. Test Tags before Publishing. Follow Privacy, Consent and Applicable Data Protection Requirements. Collect only the necessary Data.

Q31. What Are Google Alerts? Explain Their Uses.

Google Alerts

Google Alerts is a Free Notification Service from Google that can send Email Notifications when new Web Results related to selected Keywords or Topics are found. It can be used to monitor new Online Mentions of a Brand, Product, Competitor, Industry Topic or a particular Subject.

New Articles, News Pages and Public Web Content are Published on the Internet every day. Manually searching for a Topic repeatedly can be time-consuming. By setting a Keyword in Google Alerts, a Notification can be received when related new Results become available. It is useful for Online Research and Basic Brand Monitoring.

Uses of Google Alerts

Brand Monitoring:
Finding new Web Mentions of your Business or Brand Name.
Competitor Monitoring:
Monitoring new Public Web Information related to Competitors' Names or Products.
Industry News:
Receiving information about Industry Trends, Technology Updates and Market News.
Content Research:
Helping discover Blog Topics and Article Ideas.
Personal Name Monitoring:
Viewing new Web Mentions of your Name or Public Professional Work.
Product Updates:
Finding new Articles related to a Product, Service or Technology.




How to Set Up Google Alerts

  1. Open the Google Alerts Website.
  2. Enter your Keyword or Topic in the Search Box.
  3. In “Show Options”, choose available Options such as Frequency, Sources, Language, Region and Result Quantity.
  4. Choose the Delivery Email and Create the Alert.
  5. The Alert can be Edited or Deleted later.

Example

If an Educational Website needs new information about “Digital Marketing Trends”, it can create an Alert for this Keyword. When Google finds a related new Web Result, an Email Notification may be sent according to the selected Frequency.

Advantages

Google Alerts is a simple and Free Service. It helps find new Web Mentions, save time in Research and monitor Industry Updates. Separate Alerts can be created for different Keywords.

Limitations

Google Alerts does not Track every Page on the Internet or every Social Media Post. There is no Guarantee that Notifications will be received for all Mentions. The Quality and Frequency of Search Results can change. Therefore, it is not a replacement for a Complete Social Listening or Media Monitoring Platform.

Best Practices

Choose Specific Phrases instead of very Broad Keywords. Create Alerts for Product Names and Common Variations along with the Brand Name. Set the Email Frequency according to your needs. Read the Results, check their Relevance and separate incorrect or Duplicate Mentions.

Q32. What is UTM? How Would You Configure UTM Parameters?

UTM

UTM stands for Urchin Tracking Module. UTM Parameters are Tracking Codes added to the end of a Website URL to identify which Campaign, Source or Medium a Visitor came from. Google Analytics can use these Parameters to Categorize and Analyze Campaign Traffic.

If a Business Shares the same Website Link in Email, Facebook and an Advertisement, it needs to know how many Visitors came from each Medium. UTM Parameters give each Campaign Link a separate identity. This makes Traffic Acquisition and Campaign Performance Analysis easier.

Main UTM Parameters

utm_source:
Identifies the Traffic Source, such as google, facebook or newsletter.
utm_medium:
Identifies the Marketing Medium, such as cpc, email, social or referral.
utm_campaign:
Identifies the Campaign Name, such as diwali_sale or admission_2026.
utm_term:
Can be used to identify a Keyword or Term in Paid Search.
utm_content:
Useful for distinguishing different Ads or Links in the same Campaign, such as header_link or footer_banner.



How to Configure UTM Parameters

  1. First, choose the Destination URL of your Website.
  2. Then decide the Source, Medium and Campaign Name.
  3. Add Term and Content as needed.
  4. Enter the information in Google Analytics Campaign URL Builder or another reliable URL Builder.
  5. Copy the Generated URL and use it in the relevant Email, Social Post or Advertisement.

Example of a UTM URL

Suppose the Website Link is https://example.com/course and it needs to be Shared in an Email Newsletter. The Parameters utm_source=newsletter, utm_medium=email and utm_campaign=admission_2026 can be added. Parameters in a URL generally begin with a Question Mark (?) and are separated by an Ampersand (&). For example:
https://example.com/course?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=admission_2026

How to View UTM Data in GA4

1. After visiting the Website by clicking the UTM Link, open Acquisition Reports in GA4.
2. View Data in the Traffic Acquisition Report using Dimensions such as Session Source/Medium and Session Campaign.
3. Different Campaigns can be compared using the Campaign Name.

Best Practices

Maintain a Consistent Naming Convention for Source, Medium and Campaign Names. Using lowercase letters and Underscores instead of Spaces can be useful. Do not use different Spellings for the same Campaign. Generally, do not add Campaign UTMs to Internal Website Links because this can affect Original Acquisition Attribution.

Common Mistakes

Common mistakes include incorrect Spelling in UTM Parameters, Inconsistent Names, an incorrect Medium, Parameters being removed during Redirects and an incorrect Destination URL. Never include Sensitive Personal Information in UTM Parameters because the URL may be visible or Shared in many places.

Advantages

UTM Tracking helps identify Campaign Sources, compare Email and Social Campaigns, Analyze Paid Promotion and make Marketing Budget Decisions. It helps Marketers understand which Campaign Links are bringing Website Traffic.
